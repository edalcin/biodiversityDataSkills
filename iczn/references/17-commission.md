# Chapter 17 — The International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature (Articles 77–84)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/ · 4th edition 1999

This chapter defines the Commission itself: where its authority comes from, what it is empowered to do, and what its published acts (Declarations, Opinions, Lists, Indexes) legally mean. Land here when a case outcome conflicts with the automatic result the rest of the Code would produce — the Commission is the mechanism by which the Code's own default outcomes (priority, homonymy, etc.) can be overridden in a named, citable way. The second half of this file ("Working with the Commission — practice") is an original operational guide, not a paraphrase of the Code, for a taxonomist deciding whether to file a Case.

## Article 77 — Relation of Commission to international bodies from which it derives functions and powers
Establishes the chain of delegated authority behind the Commission; not itself a rule a taxonomist applies to a name.

- **77.1** — The Commission is a permanent body; its powers and Constitution derive from resolutions of the International Congresses of Zoology and their delegated successors, not from any national authority. [Art. 77.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-77-relation-of-commission-to-international-bodies-from-which-it-derives-functions-and-powers/#art-77-1)
- **77.2** — The XVII International Congress of Zoology (1972) delegated these powers to the International Union of Biological Sciences (IUBS), including IUBS's own power to further delegate under conditions set here. [Art. 77.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-77-relation-of-commission-to-international-bodies-from-which-it-derives-functions-and-powers/#art-77-2)
- **77.3.1–77.3.5** — Any further delegation requires a Section of Zoological Nomenclature to be constituted to elect Commission members and review Code/Constitution amendment proposals; the receiving body must adopt the provisions; the Commission must concur in advance; the Commission may terminate a delegation that is not being carried out and transfer it elsewhere; any such Commission decision needs a two-thirds secret postal ballot. [Art. 77.3](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-77-relation-of-commission-to-international-bodies-from-which-it-derives-functions-and-powers/#art-77-3)
- **77.4** — The Commission operates under a Constitution (see Art. 84 below). [Art. 77.4](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-77-relation-of-commission-to-international-bodies-from-which-it-derives-functions-and-powers/#art-77-4)
- **77.5** — If a delegation is terminated, the Commission continues to function under the Code and Constitution during the transition and reports to whatever body inherits authority, filling vacancies by the casual-vacancy procedure in the meantime. [Art. 77.5](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-77-relation-of-commission-to-international-bodies-from-which-it-derives-functions-and-powers/#art-77-5)

## Article 78 — Powers and duties of the Commission
The operative grant of authority: the plenary power, the List of Available Names in Zoology, the power to rule on individual cases, and the Commission's standing duties.

- **78.1** — The Commission holds the "plenary power" (granted by the IX International Congress of Zoology, 1913, and ratified in every subsequent Code): under the conditions of Art. 81, it may suspend the Code's application in a particular case for any provision **except** those in Chapters 17 and 18 themselves. Rulings under the plenary power are published as Opinions. [Art. 78.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-1) — **2012 electronic-publication amendment touched this Article's surrounding provisions** (see 78.2.4 below).
- **78.2.1** — The Commission may establish the List of Available Names in Zoology (LAN) and adopt Parts of it under the Art. 79 procedure. [Art. 78.2.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-2-1)
- **78.2.2** — Where an Article requires an author to refer a matter to the Commission, the Commission decides it and publishes an Opinion. [Art. 78.2.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-2-2)
- **78.2.3** — On its own initiative or on referral, the Commission may interpret or apply the Code to any nomenclatural question and rule by Opinion. [Art. 78.2.3](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-2-3)
- **78.2.4** *(added by the 2012 electronic-publication amendment)* — The Commission may establish and maintain an Official Register of Zoological Nomenclature (ZooBank), electronic or paper, to record works, names, and nomenclatural acts; the Official Lists and Indexes may be maintained within it. This is the constitutional basis for ZooBank — see `references/zoobank.md` for registration mechanics. [Art. 78.2.4](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-2-4)
- **78.3.1** — The Commission considers proposed amendments to the Code under its Constitution's procedures. [Art. 78.3.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-3-1)
- **78.3.2** — If two-thirds of votes cast determine a proposed amendment is a mere clarification (not a major change), the Commission may issue a **Declaration** — a provisional amendment to the Code, in force subject to Art. 80.1 until ratified or rejected by the international body having authority (Art. 77). [Art. 78.3.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-3-2)
- **78.3.3** — The Commission may **not** issue a Declaration for a proposal that would be a major change; that requires the full Art. 90 amendment procedure. [Art. 78.3.3](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-3-3)
- **78.4.1–78.4.5** — Standing duties: consider applications to review a prior Commission decision; enter ruled-on names/works in the Official Lists and Indexes (including Official Corrections); report on nomenclatural matters through the *Bulletin of Zoological Nomenclature*; report to the international body with authority over it (Art. 77); and discharge other duties that body assigns in consultation with the Commission. [Art. 78.4](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-78-powers-and-duties-of-the-commission/#art-78-4)

## Article 79 — List of Available Names in Zoology
Defines the LAN mechanism: a curated positive list for a taxonomic field that, once adopted, becomes the sole source of availability for that field.

- **79 (chapeau)** — An international body of zoologists (e.g. a Congress, society, or IUBS Scientific Member), in consultation with the Commission, may propose that the Commission adopt a Part of the LAN for a major taxonomic field. [Art. 79](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-79-list-of-available-names-in-zoology/#art-79)
- **79.1.1–79.1.5** — The proposed Part must specify its taxonomic/rank/date scope; for each name, give its bibliographic reference, authorship, date, status (and precedence if it differs from priority), name-bearing type details, any prior Commission ruling on it, and how homonymy outside the Part's scope was resolved. [Art. 79.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-79-list-of-available-names-in-zoology/#art-79-1)
- **79.2** — Adoption follows a multi-year formal process: a Commission ad hoc committee is appointed to consult with proposers; the initial proposal is published in the BZN with a 12-month comment period; a revised proposal (excluding any name established less than 5 years before the initial submission) follows at least two years later, itself republished for comment; the Commission then votes to adopt or abandon under its plenary-power voting procedures. [Art. 79.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-79-list-of-available-names-in-zoology/#art-79-2)
- **79.3.1–79.3.3** — A Part becomes effective on the date the Commission's adoption notice is published in the BZN; the Commission must first confirm the Part is publicly accessible; the notice fixes the Part's title and scope. [Art. 79.3](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-79-list-of-available-names-in-zoology/#art-79-3)
- **79.4.1–79.4.3** — **This is the consequential rule.** Once a Part is adopted: (1) a name in it is deemed available with the spelling, date and authorship recorded, regardless of contrary evidence; (2) the nominal taxon it denotes is deemed to have the type recorded in the List, regardless of contrary evidence; and (3) **any name within the Part's scope that is NOT listed has no status whatsoever in zoological nomenclature, however available it was before** — the List becomes the exhaustive and exclusive source of availability for that scope. [Art. 79.4](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-79-list-of-available-names-in-zoology/#art-79-4) — **Rec. 79A** advises that an author citing a name excluded from the List for historical purposes should flag that it has no current nomenclatural status.
- **79.5.1–79.5.2** — The Commission may, using the plenary power (Art. 81) and only for exceptional confusion, amend a List entry by Opinion; the amendment takes effect from BZN publication. This does not block an author from making a first type-species designation for a pre-1931 genus-group name, or a lectotype/neotype designation under Arts. 74–75, for a name already in the List; such subsequent fixations may be inserted into the List. [Art. 79.5](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-79-list-of-available-names-in-zoology/#art-79-5) — **Rec. 79B** asks authors to inform the Commission promptly of such lectotype/neotype designations.
- **79.6** — The Commission may, in exceptional circumstances and using the plenary power, add an omitted-but-previously-available name to an adopted Part, restoring its availability, recorded in an Opinion. [Art. 79.6](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-79-list-of-available-names-in-zoology/#art-79-6)
- **Database relevance:** No LAN Part has in practice been adopted under the 1999 Code as of this writing — the mechanism exists but is dormant. A data model should not assume LAN gating by default; if a taxonomic group of interest is later brought under a LAN Part, availability must be re-derived from the List, not from ordinary Art. 10–20 rules, for names in that scope.

## Article 80 — Status of actions of the Commission
Specifies the legal weight of each kind of Commission publication.

- **80.1** — A **Declaration** has the force of a provisional Code amendment and stays in force until the authorizing international body (Art. 77) ratifies or rejects it; if ratified, the Code is deemed amended from the Declaration's date. [Art. 80.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-1)
- **80.2** — For a case about an individual work/name/act, the Commission rules by **Opinion**, either (80.2.1) stating how the Code applies/is interpreted, or (80.2.2) using the plenary power to exempt the case from the Code and prescribe the course to follow. [Art. 80.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-2)
- **80.3** — An Opinion takes effect immediately on BZN publication. [Art. 80.3](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-3)
- **80.4** — Genuine errors/omissions in an Opinion (bibliographic slip, lapsus calami, a missed Official List/Index entry) may be corrected by the Commission without a fresh vote — unless the error negates the ruling itself, in which case the Commission must reconsider and issue a further Opinion. [Art. 80.4](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-4)
- **80.5** — An Opinion is construed narrowly: it applies only to the case actually before the Commission; no conclusion beyond what it expressly states may be drawn from it. [Art. 80.5](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-5)
- **80.6.1–80.6.5** — For names in an **Official List**: entry means the name is available (80.6.1); its further status (valid/synonym etc.) follows the ruling Opinion plus normal Code application, except that a contrary LAN status controls (80.6.2, cf. 80.8); a name can be listed without qualification (80.6.3); relative precedence among Officially Listed synonyms follows normal Code rules unless the Commission rules otherwise (80.6.4); and a work on the Official List of Works Approved as Available is subject to the Code and to any Commission-imposed limitation on its use (80.6.5). [Art. 80.6](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-6)
- **80.7.1–80.7.2** — For names/works in an **Official Index**: the entry carries whatever status the ruling gave it (80.7.1); a name or act in an Indexed work **has no availability or validity** unless the Commission rules otherwise by plenary power, though the work may still be cited as a source of information unless the Commission has ruled it unpublished (80.7.2). [Art. 80.7](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-7)
- **80.8** — If the LAN, an Official List, and an Opinion give a name contradictory status, the **LAN status controls** unless the Commission has ruled otherwise. [Art. 80.8](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-8)
- **80.9** — No prior Commission ruling on a particular work/name/act can be set aside without the Commission's own consent — a later author cannot simply relitigate it. [Art. 80.9](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-80-status-of-actions-of-the-commission/#art-80-9)

## Article 81 — Use of the Plenary Power
Sets the actual scope and guiding principles for the Commission's power to override the Code in a specific case.

- **81.1** — On due notice under its Constitution, the Commission may modify the Code's application to a particular case if normal application would, in its judgment, disturb stability/universality or cause confusion. It may conserve, totally/partially/conditionally suppress, or give specified precedence to any name, type fixation, other nomenclatural act, or publication, and establish replacements. **Limit:** the plenary power cannot touch Chapters 17–18 themselves (Art. 78.1); within that bound it reaches essentially all of Chapters 1–16, including — where the Commission judges it necessary for stability — displacing the ordinary operation of the Principle of Priority in an individual case (that is the entire point of "suppression" and "precedence" rulings). [Art. 81.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-81-use-of-the-plenary-power/#art-81-1)
- **81.2.1** — "Total suppression" of an older homonym removes it for both the Principle of Priority and the Principle of Homonymy, freeing the later homonym for valid use; a totally suppressed species-group name nonetheless remains available (Art. 10.6) and can still serve as a type species (Art. 67.1.2). [Art. 81.2.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-81-use-of-the-plenary-power/#art-81-2-1)
- **81.2.2** — "Partial suppression" of an older objective synonym removes it only for Priority, leaving it available and still capable of blocking as a homonym. [Art. 81.2.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-81-use-of-the-plenary-power/#art-81-2-2)
- **81.2.3.1–81.2.3.2** — An older subjective synonym may instead be "conditionally suppressed": usable only when the two taxa are treated as distinct, or when the older name is the valid name of a taxon subordinate to and included within the taxon denoted by the later name (e.g. a genus used as a subgenus). See Art. 81.2.3 for the *Argynnis*/*Argyreus* worked example (Opinion 161, 1945) on the site. [Art. 81.2.3](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-81-use-of-the-plenary-power/#art-81-2-3)
- **81.2.4** — If the Commission declines to use the plenary power in a case, its Opinion must still specify which name(s) are to be used and any action to be taken. [Art. 81.2.4](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-81-use-of-the-plenary-power/#art-81-2-4)

## Article 82 — Status of case under consideration
Governs what happens to a name's usage while a Case is pending.

- **82.1** — While a case is under Commission consideration, **prevailing usage** (as defined in the Glossary — see `references/glossary.md`) of the affected names must be maintained until the Commission publishes its ruling. Practically: an author must not pre-empt the outcome by unilaterally switching usage, publishing a "corrective" replacement, or treating the outcome as settled. [Art. 82.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-82-status-of-case-under-consideration/#art-82-1)
- **82.2** — A case is deemed under consideration from the date the BZN publishes notice that it has been received — not from the date it was submitted. [Art. 82.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-82-status-of-case-under-consideration/#art-82-2)

## Article 83 — Obligations and discretion of the Commission
- **83** — The Commission has no obligation to search out Code violations, to verify or supplement information in applications submitted to it, or to initiate action on its own within its competence — though it may do any of these at its discretion. Practically: the burden of a complete, well-documented Case rests on the applicant. [Art. 83](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-83-obligations-and-discretion-of-the-commission/#art-83)

## Article 84 — Constitution and Bylaws
- **84** — Membership, Officers, Council, elections, voting procedures, meetings and related governance matters are set out in the Commission's Constitution and Bylaws, not the Code itself. [Art. 84](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-84-constitution-and-bylaws/#art-84)
- **84.1** — The Constitution can be amended only by the same procedure as the Code (Art. 90). [Art. 84.1](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-84-constitution-and-bylaws/#art-84-1)
- **84.2** — Bylaws can be made, amended, and suspended by the Commission itself under procedures the Constitution sets. [Art. 84.2](https://code.iczn.org/the-international-commission-on-zoological-nomenclature/article-84-constitution-and-bylaws/#art-84-2)

**Governance structure (Constitution, last amended 2022 — NOT part of the 1999 Code text; see [Constitution of the ICZN](https://code.iczn.org/appendices/constitution-of-the-iczn/)):** ~18 members serving staggered terms (normally up to 18 years, 24 for a President), elected by the Section of Zoological Nomenclature of IUBS; governed by a President, Vice-President and Council; votes on ordinary matters need a simple majority, votes on plenary-power use or Code/Constitution amendments need two-thirds of votes cast in a three-month secret postal ballot preceded by published notice. This is administrative background, not legislative text — cite the Constitution itself, never this summary, in any procedural dispute.

## Working with the Commission — practice

*Everything below this line is operational guidance, not a restatement of the Code's legislative text. It is grounded in Arts. 77–83 above plus the Commission's own public-facing channels; nothing here should be cited as an Article.*

**What a Case is.** A Case is a formal application asking the Commission to use its powers under Art. 78 (most often the plenary power, Art. 81) to resolve a nomenclatural problem that the Code's automatic rules would otherwise decide in a way that damages stability or causes confusion — e.g. reviving a long-forgotten senior synonym, or leaving two established names in unresolved homonymy with no available replacement. Applications are published for comment in the ***Bulletin of Zoological Nomenclature*** (BZN, ISSN 0007-5167), the Commission's own journal (Art. 78.4.3, Art. 79.2.2.1, Art. 82.2 all route through it). The ICZN's website, **https://www.iczn.org/**, is the authoritative current source for how to submit a Case, required format, and any fees — this file deliberately does not state submission mechanics, since they are set by Commission procedure, not the Code, and change independently of it. What every application must substantively argue, regardless of current form requirements: the precise nomenclatural problem; full bibliographic details of the name(s) and act(s) involved; the specific ruling requested (e.g. "suppress name X under Art. 81 in favour of Y"); and why ordinary application of the Code would disturb stability or universality (the Art. 81.1 test the Commission actually applies).

**What an Opinion is.** An Opinion is the Commission's published, binding ruling on a submitted Case (Art. 80.2). It takes effect on BZN publication (Art. 80.3), is construed narrowly to the case decided (Art. 80.5), and cannot be set aside by a later author without the Commission's own consent (Art. 80.9). Cite an Opinion as **"Opinion \<number\> (\<year\>)"** — e.g. Opinion 161 (1945), the *Argynnis*/*Argyreus* case referenced under Art. 81.2.3 above.

**Declarations 44–47** — the instrument (Art. 78.3.2, Art. 78.3.3) by which the Commission issues a provisional amendment to clarify (not majorly change) the Code. All four are in force and already integrated into the current online Code text:
- **Declaration 44** — amended Art. 74.7.3, concerning fixation of a lectotype or neotype in electronic works and interaction with the 2012 electronic-publication provisions. See `references/16-species-group-types.md`.
- **Declaration 45** — amended the Recommendations under Art. 73 and the Glossary definition of "specimen, preserved," clarifying what counts as a preserved specimen for name-bearing type purposes. See `references/13-typification.md`.
- **Declaration 46** — amended Art. 8.8 and the Glossary definition of "retraction," clarifying when a published act is treated as retracted for nomenclatural purposes. See `references/03-publication.md`.
- **Declaration 47** — amended the Examples under Art. 13.1.1 to address molecular (DNA sequence) data in the context of establishing availability of names published after 1930. See `references/04-availability.md`.

**When to file a Case rather than act unilaterally** — the Code itself names several junctures where an author's own action is capped and the matter must instead go to the Commission:
- **Art. 23.9.3** — reversal of precedence (nomen protectum/nomen oblitum) when the conditions of Art. 23.9.1.1–23.9.1.2 are *not* met but stability still requires displacing a rediscovered senior synonym: the Commission, not the author, must act. See `references/06-validity-priority.md`.
- **Art. 41** — a misidentified type genus of a family-group name is a matter for the Commission, not silent correction by an author. See `references/14-family-group-types.md`.
- **Art. 70.3** — a misidentified type species of a genus-group name: an author's remedy is to refer the case to the Commission rather than simply re-designate. See `references/15-genus-group-types.md`.
- **Art. 75.5, 75.8** — neotype designations that do not meet the exceptional-necessity conditions of Art. 75.3, or that conflict with a subsequently discovered original name-bearing type, require Commission adjudication. See `references/16-species-group-types.md`.
- **Homonymy with no available replacement** — where a junior homonym cannot simply be replaced (e.g. both names are in wide current use, or a replacement would itself be destabilizing), suppression of the senior homonym under Art. 81.2.1 is the Commission-mediated alternative to an author manufacturing a new replacement name. See `references/12-homonymy.md`.

**Database relevance:** Commission actions are first-class nomenclatural acts with their own citable identity (Opinion number + year + BZN volume/page) and they *override* the Code's automatically-derived outcomes for priority, homonymy, and typification. A data model that computes a name's validity purely from priority and homonymy rules will silently get every Commission-conserved or Commission-suppressed name wrong. Concretely: every name known to have been the subject of a ruling needs a `commission_opinion` (or equivalent) field distinct from its ordinary validity/synonymy fields, recording the Opinion number, year, and the specific action taken (conserved / suppressed — total, partial, or conditional / given precedence / validated), and that field must be checked and take precedence before applying default Priority/Homonymy logic. See `references/data-modeling.md` for the field-level design.
