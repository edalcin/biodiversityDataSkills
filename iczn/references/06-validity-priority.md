# Chapter 6 — Validity of Names and Nomenclatural Acts (Articles 23–24)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/ · 4th edition 1999
This chapter decides which of two or more competing available names for the same taxon is the *valid* one — the second question of every nomenclatural analysis, after Chapter 4 has settled which names are *available* at all. Land here whenever ranking synonyms or homonyms, deciding whether a long-unused senior name can be displaced by entrenched usage, or resolving names published on the very same day.

## Article 23 — Principle of Priority
States and bounds the rule that the oldest available name for a taxon is normally its valid name, and sets the narrow, procedural exceptions to that rule.

- **23.1** — The valid name of a taxon is its **oldest available name**, unless (a) that name has been invalidated by some Code provision, or (b) another name is given precedence by the Code or by a Commission ruling. Priority governs: validity among synonyms (23.3), relative precedence among homonyms (Arts. 53–60), correctness of spellings (Arts. 24, 32), and validity of nomenclatural acts such as First Reviser actions (24.2) and type fixations (Arts. 68, 69, 74.1.3, 75.4). [Art. 23.1](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-1)
  - **23.1.1** — Family-group names have their own exceptions — see `references/08-family-group.md` (Arts. 35.5, 40).
  - **23.1.2** — Disused family-group homonyms — see Art. 55.3.1.1.
  - **23.1.3** — Certain genus-group names are excluded from priority entirely — Arts. 20 and 23.7.
  - **23.1.4** — Certain species-group names are partly excluded — Arts. 23.7.3, 23.8.
- **23.2** — Priority's **purpose** is to promote nomenclatural stability. It is *not* meant to unseat a long-accepted name merely because a technically older synonym or homonym has been found (the 23.9 reversal-of-precedence mechanism exists precisely for this), nor merely because a previously unrecognized prior nomenclatural act (e.g. an earlier type fixation) has come to light (see Arts. 70.2, 75.6). [Art. 23.2](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-2)
- **23.3** — When two or more previously separate nominal taxa at the same rank (family, genus, or species group) are united into one taxon, the united taxon takes the name that priority selects among them, with a suffix change if a family-group name is involved (Art. 34). [Art. 23.3](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-3)
  - **23.3.1** — Priority is unaffected by a taxon's rank changing within its group, or by a mandatory family-group suffix change on rank change.
  - **23.3.2** — Priority applies regardless of: a body part being named before the whole animal; different life stages/forms/sexes being named as separate nominal taxa; or (pre-1931) a name based on an animal's work/trace preceding a name for the animal itself (ichnotaxa: see 23.7).
  - **23.3.3** — Priority applies to interpolated aggregate names in parenthetical position (Art. 6.2); such a name's precedence is that of the species group (see Art. 11.9.3.5).
  - **23.3.4** — Priority does **not** apply to infrasubspecific names, since they fall outside zoological nomenclature (Art. 1.3.4) — unless the name is later established for a species/subspecies (Arts. 10.2, 45.5, 45.6), at which point priority runs from that later date of availability.
  - **23.3.5** — **Effect on synonyms of coordinate ranks:** if a name in current use for a taxon is found unavailable or invalid, it is replaced by the **next-oldest available synonym**, including names of contained subordinate taxa of the same group (e.g. a subgenus name can replace an invalid genus name), provided that replacement name is not itself invalid. If no synonym qualifies, a new replacement name must be established (Art. 60.3). [Art. 23.3.5](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-3-5)
  - **23.3.6** — An available name does not stop being usable merely because it has been treated as a junior synonym; an author who judges the synonymy erroneous — or finds the senior synonym unavailable/invalid — may use it as valid again.
  - **23.3.7** — A name that is valid under priority cannot be rejected by anyone, even its own author, for being inappropriate or tautonymous, or for having an incorrect spelling (it remains valid in its *corrected* form: Art. 19).
- **23.4** — Priority likewise governs relative precedence among **homonyms**, including secondary species-group homonyms, in conjunction with the Principle of Homonymy (Art. 52); for names homonymous and published simultaneously, see Art. 24. [Art. 23.4](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-4)
  - **23.4.1** — Applies to a family-group name if either that name or its type genus's name is a junior homonym (see Arts. 55, 39).
- **23.5** — Priority applies to competing **spellings** of an available name, except where an incorrect spelling has been preserved under Art. 33.3.1 (or, for family-group names, Arts. 29.4/29.5); see also Art. 33.2.3.1 (preserved unjustified emendations). [Art. 23.5](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-5)
- **23.6** — For nomenclatural **acts** (First Reviser actions, type-species fixation, first species inclusion in a genus, lectotype/neotype designation), only the *first* such act taken is valid; family-group types are fixed automatically and not subject to a later act (Art. 63; but see Art. 16.2 for post-1999 names). [Art. 23.6](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-6)
- **23.7** — Collective groups and ichnotaxa are carved out of ordinary priority competition (Homonymy Principle still applies, Arts. 55–57):
  - **23.7.1** — A name established expressly for a collective group never competes in priority with other genus-group names.
  - **23.7.2** — A genus-group name later repurposed for a collective group stops competing in priority while so used (see Art. 67.14).
  - **23.7.3** — An ichnotaxon name never competes in priority with a name for the animal that made (or may have made) the trace — e.g. a trackway name and the trackmaker's body-fossil name can coexist without one displacing the other.
- **23.8** — A species-group name established for an animal later shown to be a **hybrid** must never become the valid name of either parent species, however old it is; it can, however, enter homonymy (see Art. 17.2 for taxa of hybrid origin). [Art. 23.8](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-8)

### Article 23.9 — Reversal of precedence
The Code's mechanism for keeping a well-entrenched junior name in use over a rediscovered but functionally forgotten senior name/homonym, in service of Art. 23.2's stability purpose.

- **23.9.1** — Prevailing usage of the junior name **must** be maintained only when **both** of the following conditions hold together — this is a strict conjunction, not either/or:
  - **23.9.1.1** — The senior synonym or homonym has **not been used as a valid name after 1899** (i.e. effectively unused as valid for well over a century up to the present). [Art. 23.9.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-9-1-1)
  - **23.9.1.2** — The junior synonym or homonym has been used, as the presumed valid name for that particular taxon, in **at least 25 works**, by **at least 10 authors**, within the **immediately preceding 50 years**, **and** that usage spans **not less than 10 years**. [Art. 23.9.1.2](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-9-1-2)

  ⚠ **Both conditions are mandatory.** Satisfying 23.9.1.1 alone (senior name simply obscure/unused) does **not** license reversal — the specific 25-works/10-authors/50-year/10-year usage threshold in 23.9.1.2 must independently be demonstrated. An author who reverses precedence on 23.9.1.1 alone, without documenting 23.9.1.2, has not validly invoked this Article.

  Casual mentions do not count toward the 23.9.1.2 tally: deliberate use contrary to 23.9.1, mere mention in a synonymy list, or a bare listing in an abstract, nomenclator, index, or other list is excluded from the usage count (**23.9.6**). [Art. 23.9.6](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-9-6)

- **23.9.2** — Reversal is an **act that must be published**, never a private or implicit conclusion. The discovering author must: cite both names together; explicitly state the younger name is valid, invoking this Article; supply evidence that 23.9.1.2 is met; and state that, to the author's knowledge, 23.9.1.1 also holds. Precedence changes from the **date that act is published**. When cited thereafter, the younger (valid) name may be qualified as **nomen protectum**, and the older (invalid) name as **nomen oblitum** — note the terminology is reversed from intuition: the *protected* name is the **junior** one, and the *forgotten* name is the **senior** one. [Art. 23.9.2](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-9-2) (See `references/glossary.md` for both terms.)
  - **Rec. 23A** (advisory) — If the author would prefer outright suppression of the older name rather than a mere precedence swap, they should additionally petition the Commission for a ruling, alongside taking action under 23.9.2.
- **23.9.3** — If the 23.9.1 conditions are **not** met, but an author still believes using the older name would threaten stability/universality or cause confusion, the case **must be referred to the Commission** for a plenary-power ruling (Art. 81); prevailing (junior-name) usage is maintained while the case is pending (Art. 82). See `references/17-commission.md` for the referral and plenary-power procedure. [Art. 23.9.3](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-9-3)
- **23.9.4** — For family-group homonymy arising from similar-but-not-identical type-genus names, see Art. 55.3 instead.
- **23.9.5** — **Special case — junior primary homonyms treated as different taxa after 1899:** if a species-group name is discovered to be a junior primary homonym (Art. 53.3) of another name, but the two apply to taxa not considered congeneric after 1899, the author must **not** automatically replace the junior homonym; the case goes to the Commission for a plenary-power ruling, and prevailing usage of **both** names is maintained meanwhile (Art. 82). [Art. 23.9.5](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-9-5)
- **23.9.6** — See usage-counting exclusions folded into 23.9.1.2 above.
- **23.10** — **Erroneous reversal:** if a published 23.9.2 act is later found to have been based on conditions that were not actually met, the case must go to the Commission; prevailing usage stays as-is (junior name) until the Commission rules — an author discovering the error does not get to unilaterally revert to the senior name. [Art. 23.10](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-10)
- **23.11** — An author who wants strict priority applied (reinstating the older name) **despite** the 23.9.1 conditions being met must seek a Commission plenary-power ruling — reversal-eligibility does not force reversal; departing from it also needs Commission sign-off. [Art. 23.11](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-11)
- **23.12** — Transitional rule for names rejected 6 Nov 1961–1 Jan 1973 under the then-current "Article 23b" *nomen oblitum* provision: such a rejection stands (junior synonym keeps precedence) unless the Commission rules otherwise. "Rejected" is construed strictly — the name must have been explicitly cited and passed over for a junior synonym, not merely disregarded (23.12.1). A name rejected under old Art. 23b may be used as valid again if it is no longer considered a synonym of anything, or if its synonyms are themselves invalid (23.12.2). [Art. 23.12](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-23-principle-of-priority/#art-23-12)

**Database relevance:** A priority/reversal outcome (which name is currently valid, which is *nomen protectum*/*nomen oblitum*) is a **taxonomic opinion attached to a source** — an assertion with an author and a publication date (the 23.9.2 act) — not an immutable fact permanently stamped on the name row. Model it as a versioned assertion (name pair, asserting author, publication, date, direction of precedence), never as a bare boolean/status flag on the name record, because a later Commission ruling (23.9.3, 23.10, 23.11) or a fresh 23.9.2 act can supersede it. See `references/data-modeling.md` for the assertion-vs-fact pattern.

**Common errors:** Applying only 23.9.1.1 (senior name looks obscure) while skipping the 23.9.1.2 usage count is the most frequent invalid reversal in practice — the Article requires both, conjunctively. Also: treating a private judgment that a name "should" be a nomen oblitum as sufficient, when 23.9.2 requires a published, explicit act citing both names.

## Article 24 — Precedence between simultaneously published names, spellings, or acts
Resolves ties: names, spellings, or nomenclatural acts that were established on the exact same date, where priority-by-date cannot distinguish them.

- **24.1** — **Automatic determination:** if homonyms or synonyms are established simultaneously but at *different ranks* within the same group (family, genus, or species group), the name proposed at the **higher rank automatically takes precedence** — no reviser action needed (see Arts. 55.5, 56.3, 57.7). For simultaneous but differing type fixations for a taxon and its nominotypical subordinate taxon, see Art. 61.2.1. [Art. 24.1](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-24-precedence-between-simultaneously-published-names-spellings-or-acts/#art-24-1)
- **24.2** — **Determination by the First Reviser**, used whenever precedence cannot be fixed objectively (i.e. 24.1 does not resolve it):
  - **24.2.1** — Statement of the **Principle of the First Reviser**: precedence is fixed by whichever author first, in a published work, cites the competing names/acts together and selects between them; that author is the "First Reviser". [Art. 24.2.1](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-24-precedence-between-simultaneously-published-names-spellings-or-acts/#art-24-2-1)
  - **24.2.2** — Applies to two or more names (identical or different, same or different types) or two or more nomenclatural acts published on the same date, in the same or different works — the First Reviser's choice fixes precedence, unless Art. 24.1 already settled it automatically. [Art. 24.2.2](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-24-precedence-between-simultaneously-published-names-spellings-or-acts/#art-24-2-2)
  - **24.2.3** — **Selection of correct original spelling:** if a work itself spells a name more than one way, the first author to cite those spellings together and pick one as correct is the First Reviser for spelling; the chosen spelling (if not itself incorrect under Art. 32.4/32.5) becomes the fixed correct original spelling, and every other variant becomes unavailable (Art. 32.4). [Art. 24.2.3](https://code.iczn.org/validity-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/article-24-precedence-between-simultaneously-published-names-spellings-or-acts/#art-24-2-3)
  - **24.2.4** — **Special case — an author acting as their own First Reviser:** if the (or a joint) original author of two spelling variants later uses one of them as valid in a subsequent work (including a corrigendum), and no formal First Reviser choice preceded it, that original author is deemed the First Reviser — regardless of whether both variants are cited together — and the spelling they used becomes the correct original spelling.
  - **24.2.5** — A First Reviser act is **nullified** if it is later shown that precedence could in fact be determined objectively all along (i.e. 24.1 or other Code provisions actually applied).

**Recommendations:** Rec. 24A (advisory — a First Reviser should choose whichever option best serves nomenclatural stability and universality) · Rec. 24B (advisory — when choosing between identical names published same-day, follow contemporary attributions of authorship per Art. 50.6). Both advisory only, not binding on the First Reviser's choice.

**Database relevance:** Where Art. 24.2 applies, record the First Reviser's identity, publication, and date as part of the precedence assertion (same pattern as the Art. 23.9 reversal record above) — the tie-break is itself a citable nomenclatural act, not a derivable fact.
