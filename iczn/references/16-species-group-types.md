# Chapter 16 — Types in the Species Group (Articles 71–76)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/ · 4th edition 1999

This chapter governs the specimens that objectively anchor species-group names: what a type series is, which of its members carry the name (are "name-bearing"), how holotypes, syntypes, lectotypes and neotypes are fixed or designated, and how the type locality is derived from the name-bearing type. A reader lands here to determine whether a given specimen is nomenclaturally load-bearing, whether a purported lectotype or neotype designation is actually valid, or how to model type status and type locality in a specimen database. Article numbers here are the ones a manuscript reviewer or curator cites directly against a paper's "Material examined" section.

## Article 71 — Application
Governs scope: this chapter's provisions apply equally to species and to subspecies, including taxa treated as subspecific under the exception for species-group names published in a genus/species combination that is later treated as subspecific.

- **71.1** — Everything in Chapter 16 applies identically to nominal species and nominal subspecies, including taxa deemed subspecific.
  [Art. 71](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-71-application/#art-71-1) · cf. [Art. 45.6](../06-validity-priority.md)

## Article 72 — General provisions
Defines the vocabulary of the chapter: type series, name-bearing type, the categories of "other specimens," what may belong to a type series, and what is eligible to serve as a name-bearing type at all.

- **72.1** — "Type" is an umbrella word taxonomists attach to many kinds of specimens; the Code regulates only three categories.
  [Art. 72.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-1)
  - **72.1.1** — *Type series*: every specimen on which the author established the nominal species-group taxon, minus any excluded under 72.4.1. If no holotype was designated and no lectotype has since been fixed, every member of the type series is a syntype and the series collectively is the name-bearing type.
    [Art. 72.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-1-1)
  - **72.1.2** — *Name-bearing types*: specimens that carry the name — holotype (Art. 73.1), syntypes (Art. 73.2), lectotype (Art. 74) or neotype (Art. 75).
    [Art. 72.1.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-1-2)
  - **72.1.3** — *Other specimens*: paratypes and paralectotypes — members of the type series (or former syntypes) with no name-bearing function.
    [Art. 72.1.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-1-3)
- **Rec. 72A** *(advisory)* — "Allotype" (a specimen of opposite sex to the holotype) is a usable label but is not a Code category and has no name-bearing function.
- **72.2** — For taxa established before 2000, the name-bearing type may be fixed either at the original publication (Art. 73) or subsequently (Art. 74); a neotype is possible only if no name-bearing type is believed extant (Art. 75).
  [Art. 72.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-2)
- **72.3** — For taxa established after 1999 (other than a nomen novum), original fixation of either a holotype or syntypes is mandatory at publication; for syntypes, only specimens the author expressly indicates as the basis of the new taxon count.
  [Art. 72.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-3) → interacts with Art. 16.4
- **72.4** — *Type series*: consists of every specimen the author included in the new taxon (directly or by bibliographic reference).
  [Art. 72.4](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4)
  - **72.4.1** — Excluded from the type series: specimens the author expressly excludes (per 72.4.6), specimens referred to as distinct variants (by name, letter or number), and specimens only doubtfully attributed to the taxon.
    [Art. 72.4.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-1)
    - **72.4.1.1** — For taxa established before 2000, any evidence (published or unpublished) may be used to determine type-series membership.
      [Art. 72.4.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-1-1)
  - **72.4.2** — If the new taxon is based wholly or partly on an earlier author's published misidentification, the type series is (or includes) the misidentified specimen(s), however cited.
    [Art. 72.4.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-2)
  - **72.4.3** — For a name first published as a junior synonym but made available before 1961 under Art. 11.6, the type series is the specimen(s) cited in that synonymy, or, absent a citation, whatever was denoted when the name was adopted as valid.
    [Art. 72.4.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-3)
  - **72.4.4** — For a name made available by bibliographic reference to a description tied to an unavailable name, the type series is (or includes) the specimen(s) that the unavailable name denoted.
    [Art. 72.4.4](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-4)
  - **72.4.5** — Once an author designates a holotype, every other type-series specimen becomes a paratype, not a syntype. Paratypes cannot be used for lectotype selection if the holotype is lost, though they remain eligible for neotype selection.
    [Art. 72.4.5](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-5)
  - **72.4.6** — If the author names "syntypes"/"cotypes"/"types" collectively, or "holotype and paratypes" together, and separately lists further specimens, that separate listing expressly excludes those extra specimens from the type series.
    [Art. 72.4.6](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-6)
  - **72.4.7** — A bare "Type" label in a later work, an unpublished museum catalogue, or a specimen label is not by itself evidence of formal type status.
    [Art. 72.4.7](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-4-7)
- **Rec. 72B** *(advisory)* — Authors excluding specimens from a type series should do so explicitly; partial illustration or partial numbering does not itself exclude the unillustrated/unnumbered remainder.
- **72.5** — *Eligibility as name-bearing type* — only the following qualify:
  [Art. 72.5](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-5)
  - **72.5.1** — an animal, part of an animal, or fossilized/extant work-product of an animal (work-product only eligible if the name was established before 1931).
  - **72.5.2** — a colony formed by asexual/vegetative multiplication from one individual (e.g. a coral colony), or part of one.
  - **72.5.3** — for fossils, a natural replacement, natural impression, natural mould, or natural cast.
  - **72.5.4** — for extant protistans, a hapantotype — one or more preparations from directly related individuals across life-cycle stages (Art. 73.3).
  - **72.5.5** — a microscope preparation ("type slide") holding multiple individuals, provided the name-bearing specimen(s) are clearly identifiable within it.
  - **72.5.6** — For a taxon based on an illustration/description (or a bibliographic reference to one), the name-bearing type is the specimen depicted or described — never the illustration or text itself.
    [Art. 72.5.6](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-5-6)
- **Rec. 72C** *(advisory)* — On a multi-specimen type slide, mark distinctly the specimens of taxonomic importance.
- **72.6** — Serving as the name-bearing type of one nominal taxon does not bar the same specimen from also being (part of) the name-bearing type of a different nominal taxon.
  [Art. 72.6](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-6)
- **72.7** — A name expressly proposed as a *nomen novum* replacement shares its name-bearing type with the earlier name it replaces — regardless of any simultaneous restriction, differing type designation, or different usage attached to the new name.
  [Art. 72.7](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-7)
- **72.8** — A nominal species and its nominotypical subspecies share the same name-bearing type.
  [Art. 72.8](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-8) · cf. Art. 47.1, 61.2
- **72.9** — Merging two or more species-group taxa into one taxonomic taxon leaves each nominal taxon's own name-bearing type unchanged; the merged taxon simply takes the oldest available name subject to priority (Art. 23, see [06-validity-priority.md](06-validity-priority.md)).
  [Art. 72.9](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-9)
- **72.10** — The name-bearing type of a nominal species-group taxon is a single specimen (holotype, lectotype, or neotype) or, where the taxon has syntypes, the series collectively; these are the objective international reference standard and must be held in trust and cared for accordingly.
  [Art. 72.10](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-72-general-provisions/#art-72-10)
- **Recs. 72D–72F** *(advisory curatorial practice)*:
  - **72D** — Label name-bearing types unmistakably.
  - **72E** — Publish all label data when designating a type.
  - **72F.1–72F.5** — Institutions holding name-bearing types should: mark them clearly, safeguard them, make them accessible for study, publish lists of their holdings, and share information on request.

## Article 73 — Name-bearing types fixed in the original publication (holotypes and syntypes)
Governs the two ways a name-bearing type can be fixed *at* original publication — a single holotype, or a series of syntypes — plus the special hapantotype category for protistans.

- **73.1** — *Holotype*: the single specimen on which a new species-group taxon is based in its original publication.
  [Art. 73.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-1)
  - **73.1.1** — Original designation: if the author states in the original publication that one specimen, and only one, is "the holotype"/"the type" or an equivalent expression, that specimen is fixed as holotype by original designation.
    [Art. 73.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-1-1)
  - **73.1.2** — Monotypy: if the taxon is based on a single specimen (stated or implied), that specimen becomes the holotype by monotypy without any express designation act. For pre-2000 taxa, evidence outside the publication may help identify which specimen (see Rec. 73F on not assuming a holotype where syntypes may in fact exist).
    [Art. 73.1.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-1-2)
  - **73.1.3** — A holotype can be fixed only in the original publication, only by the original author; a later misuse of "holotype" is instead evaluated under Art. 74.6.
    [Art. 73.1.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-1-3)
  - **73.1.4** — Designating an *illustration* of a single specimen as holotype is treated as designating the specimen itself; the specimen's loss or untraceability does not invalidate the designation.
    [Art. 73.1.4](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-1-4)
  - **73.1.5** — If a holotype composed of separate components (e.g. disarticulated parts) is later shown to derive from more than one individual, the extraneous components may be formally excluded by citation, leaving the holotype intact.
    [Art. 73.1.5](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-1-5)
- **Recs. 73A–73F** *(advisory)*:
  - **73A** — Designate the holotype in a way that facilitates future recognition.
  - **73B** — Prefer a specimen actually studied by the author over one known only from literature.
  - **73C.1–73C.10** — Publish, where relevant and known:
    - **73C.1** size (of specimen or a relevant organ/part).
    - **73C.2** full locality (incl. geographic coordinates), date, and other label data.
    - **73C.3** sex, if applicable.
    - **73C.4** developmental stage, and caste if the taxon includes more than one.
    - **73C.5** collector's name.
    - **73C.6** repository and any collection/register number assigned.
    - **73C.7** host species, for a parasite.
    - **73C.8** elevation in metres above sea level, for an extant terrestrial taxon.
    - **73C.9** depth in metres below water level, for an extant aquatic taxon.
    - **73C.10** geological age and stratigraphic position, for a fossil taxon.
  - **73D** — Label the remaining type-series specimens "paratype" once the holotype is labelled.
  - **73E** — Avoid the term "cotype" altogether.
  - **73F** — Where no holotype/syntype was ever fixed for a pre-2000 taxon that may rest on multiple specimens, proceed as if syntypes exist and designate a lectotype rather than assume a holotype.
- **Recs. 73G–73J** — **Added by Declaration 45** (in force with the 2012 electronic-publication amendment package), together with a revised Glossary definition of "specimen, preserved." All four are advisory and address the exceptional, still-discouraged-by-default practice of naming a taxon without a preserved specimen:
  - **73G** — Give detailed reasons why no preserved specimen (whole organism or part) was used as name-bearing type, and why formal naming is needed despite that.
  - **73H** — Recount due-diligence steps taken to capture or locate a physical specimen in collections.
  - **73I** — Consult specialists in the group before designating an unpreserved specimen as name-bearing type.
  - **73J** — Document diagnostic characters as completely as possible (high-resolution images, DNA sequences, etc.) when no preserved type exists.
  - None of these lowers Art. 73's mandatory requirements — an unpreserved "specimen" (e.g. a photographed live individual) can still serve as a holotype under 73.1, but Declaration 45 records the Commission's expectation that this remain rare and be justified in print.
- **73.2** — *Syntypes*: specimens of a type series that collectively constitute the name-bearing type when no holotype was designated. For a pre-2000 taxon, if neither a holotype nor a lectotype has been fixed, every type-series specimen is automatically a syntype; all syntypes have equal nomenclatural status.
  [Art. 73.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-2)
  - **73.2.1** — Syntypes may be labelled "cotype" or "type" (both read as syntype), carry no label at all, or — for pre-existing descriptions/illustrations the author adopted — never have been physically examined by the author.
    [Art. 73.2.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-2-1)
    - **73.2.1.1** — For a taxon established after 1999, only specimens the author expressly indicates as the basis of the taxon (Art. 72.3) count as syntypes — silent inclusion is not enough.
      [Art. 73.2.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-2-1-1)
  - **73.2.2** — Once a lectotype is validly designated, the remaining former syntypes permanently cease to be syntypes and become paralectotypes — non-name-bearing, and they do not revert to syntype status even if the lectotype is later lost or destroyed.
    [Art. 73.2.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-2-2) → see Art. 74.1.3 below
  - **73.2.3** — If all syntypes share one place of origin, that is the type locality; if syntypes originate from two or more localities (including different strata), the type locality spans all of them until a lectotype is designated, at which point the type locality collapses to the lectotype's own place of origin (Art. 76.2).
    [Art. 73.2.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-2-3)
- **73.3** — *Hapantotypes*: for an extant protistan taxon, one or more preparations/cultures designated collectively may serve as the holotype.
  [Art. 73.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-3)
  - **73.3.1** — A hapantotype is treated as indivisible and cannot be split by later lectotype selection.
    [Art. 73.3.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-3-1)
  - **73.3.2** — If a hapantotype is found to mix individuals of more than one species-group taxon, the extraneous individuals may be formally excluded by citation until only one taxon remains represented (parallel to holotype cleanup under Art. 73.1.5).
    [Art. 73.3.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/#art-73-3-2)

## Article 74 — Name-bearing types fixed subsequently from the type series (lectotypes from syntypes)
Governs designating one syntype, after the fact, as the sole name-bearing type — the lectotype — including the strict post-1999 formalities that make many older "lectotype" claims in the literature invalid.

- **74.1** — A lectotype may be designated from syntypes to become the sole name-bearing type and taxonomic standard (not applicable to hapantotypes, Art. 73.3).
  [Art. 74.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-1)
  - **74.1.1** — A valid lectotype designation is final: it fixes that specimen as the sole name-bearing type, and no later lectotype designation for the same taxon has any validity.
    [Art. 74.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-1-1)
  - **74.1.2** — A valid lectotype designation overrides any earlier informal restriction of how the name had been applied.
    [Art. 74.1.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-1-2)
  - **74.1.3** — A valid lectotype designation strips every other former syntype of syntype status permanently — **the remaining syntypes become paralectotypes**, with no name-bearing function.
    [Art. 74.1.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-1-3)
- **74.2** — If it is later shown that the specimen designated as lectotype was never actually a syntype, it loses lectotype status (the taxon then has no valid lectotype designation).
  [Art. 74.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-2)
- **74.3** — A lectotype designation must target one named taxon individually — blanket/collective designations across many taxa by a general rule (e.g. "the first-listed specimen in each of Dupont's species is the lectotype") are not valid designations for any of them.
  [Art. 74.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-3)
- **74.4** — Designating an illustration or description of a syntype as lectotype is treated as designating the depicted/described specimen itself; loss or untraceability of the specimen does not by itself invalidate the act.
  [Art. 74.4](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-4)
- **74.5** — For designations published before 2000: valid if the author used "lectotype"/an exact equivalent (e.g. "the type"), or unambiguously singled out one particular syntype as the sole name-bearing type. If the original work shows the taxon rested on more than one specimen, a later, wrongly-used "holotype" does not count as a lectotype designation unless the author explicitly states they are selecting that one specimen from the type series to be the name-bearing type.
  [Art. 74.5](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-5)
- **74.6** — If a taxon was long treated as based on a single specimen, and it later emerges the original description actually rested on more than one, the *first* author to have published (before 2000) the single-specimen assumption is deemed to have thereby designated that specimen as lectotype.
  [Art. 74.6](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-6)
  - **74.6.1** — That inference may rest on reference to an illustration/description (Art. 74.4), and must itself be individual to one taxon (Art. 74.3).
    [Art. 74.6.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-6-1)
- **74.7** — **Lectotype designations published after 1999** are valid only if they satisfy all three of the following:
  [Art. 74.7](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-7)
  - **74.7.1** — use the term "lectotype" or an exact translation (e.g. "lectotypus") — "the type" does not qualify.
    [Art. 74.7.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-7-1)
  - **74.7.2** — contain information sufficient to ensure the designated specimen is recognizable.
    [Art. 74.7.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-7-2)
  - **74.7.3** — contain an express statement of *deliberate designation*; merely listing a specimen as "lectotype: specimen X" is insufficient, but phrasing like "lectotype hereby designated" or "I choose specimen X as lectotype" satisfies it.
    [Art. 74.7.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-74-name-bearing-types-fixed-subsequently-from-the-type-series-lectotypes-from-syntypes/#art-74-7-3)
  - **A post-1999 designation that fails any one of 74.7.1–74.7.3 is not valid** — the purported lectotype has no name-bearing status and the taxon remains syntype-based until a compliant designation is published. This is a frequent trap in recent revisionary literature: papers that identify a specimen clearly enough but never state the act is deliberate fail 74.7.3 even though the specimen is unambiguous.
  - **Amended by Declaration 44.** The 1999 text of 74.7.3 instead required "an express statement of the taxonomic purpose of the designation." Declaration 44 replaced that wording with the current "deliberate designation" requirement above, and simultaneously added the worked Example at Art. 74.7 and **Recommendation 74G**. A designation drafted to satisfy the pre-amendment "taxonomic purpose" wording should be checked against the current 74.7.3 text, since the two are not textually identical.
- **Recs. 74A–74F** *(advisory)*:
  - **74A** — Weight prior taxonomic restrictions heavily when choosing a lectotype, for nomenclatural stability.
  - **74B** — Prefer an already-illustrated syntype, other things being equal.
  - **74C** — Publish the same data as Rec. 73C for the lectotype, plus distinguishing individual characters.
  - **74D** — Prefer syntypes held in public institutions, especially the one holding most of the series or the original author's working collection.
  - **74E** — Verify the locality where possible; prefer a known-locality syntype to one of unknown origin.
  - **74F** — Label the remaining former syntypes "paralectotype" — non-name-bearing but eligible for future neotype selection.
- **Rec. 74G** — **Added by Declaration 44.** Lectotype designation should be undertaken as part of a revisionary or other taxonomic work advancing nomenclatural stability, not merely for curatorial tidiness. Advisory, but a strong signal of the Commission's post-1999 intent behind Art. 74.7.

## Article 75 — Neotypes
Governs designating a wholly new name-bearing type when none of the original material is believed to survive — the most tightly conditioned type-fixing act in the Code, because it can redefine what a name has always meant.

- **75.1** — A neotype is the name-bearing type designated, under this Article's conditions, when no name-bearing type (holotype, lectotype, syntype, or prior neotype) is believed extant and the author judges a name-bearing type objectively necessary. Surviving paratypes or paralectotypes do not by themselves block a neotype designation.
  [Art. 75.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-1)
- **75.2** — A neotype must never be designated as an end in itself or for curatorial routine; any such designation is invalid outright, regardless of form.
  [Art. 75.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-2)
- **75.3** — **Qualifying conditions.** A neotype is validly designated only where there is an *exceptional need*, expressly stated, and the publication supplies **all** of the following particulars — the list is conjunctive; omitting any one voids the designation:
  [Art. 75.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3)
  - **75.3.1** — a statement that the designation's express purpose is clarifying the taxonomic status or the type locality of the nominal taxon.
    [Art. 75.3.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3-1)
  - **75.3.2** — a statement (or bibliographic reference to one) of the characters the author regards as differentiating the taxon from others.
    [Art. 75.3.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3-2)
  - **75.3.3** — data and description sufficient to ensure the designated specimen is recognizable.
    [Art. 75.3.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3-3)
  - **75.3.4** — the author's reasons for believing the former name-bearing type(s) lost or destroyed, and the steps taken to trace them.
    [Art. 75.3.4](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3-4)
  - **75.3.5** — evidence the neotype is consistent with what is known of the former name-bearing type from the original description and other sources (a different sex or life stage is permissible if needed for nomenclatural stability).
    [Art. 75.3.5](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3-5)
  - **75.3.6** — evidence the neotype comes as nearly as practicable from the original type locality (Art. 76.1), and, where relevant, the same geological horizon or host species as the original name-bearing type.
    [Art. 75.3.6](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3-6)
  - **75.3.7** — a statement that the neotype is, or immediately upon publication becomes, the property of a named, recognized scientific/educational institution that maintains a research collection with proper preservation facilities and provides study access.
    [Art. 75.3.7](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-3-7)
  - **A designation missing any one of 75.3.1–75.3.7, or lacking the express statement of exceptional need, is not a valid neotype designation** — the taxon remains without a name-bearing type, or retains its former one if that material is later rediscovered (Art. 75.8).
- **Recs. 75A–75B** *(advisory)*:
  - **75A** — Prefer surviving paratypes/paralectotypes as neotype material unless data, condition, or mixed-taxon risk argue otherwise, favoring topotypic specimens from the original type series.
  - **75B** — Consult specialists before designating, to avoid serious objection.
- **75.4** — Priority: the first neotype designation published under this Article's provisions for a given taxon is valid; no later designation has any effect except one made by the Commission under its plenary power (Art. 78.1; see [17-commission.md](17-commission.md)).
  [Art. 75.4](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-4)
  - **75.4.1** — If a validly designated neotype is itself lost or destroyed, any replacement neotype must independently satisfy all of Art. 75.3.
    [Art. 75.4.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-4-1)
- **75.5** — If the existing name-bearing type cannot resolve the taxon's identity (the name is a *nomen dubium*) and stability/universality are threatened, the author may petition the Commission to set the existing type aside under its plenary power (Art. 81) and designate a neotype — this is a Commission action, not something an author can do unilaterally.
  [Art. 75.5](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-5)
- **75.6** — If the existing name-bearing type conflicts with prevailing usage and stability/universality are threatened, the author should seek conservation of prevailing usage (Art. 82) by petitioning the Commission to set the type aside and designate a neotype.
  [Art. 75.6](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-6)
- **75.7** — A neotype designation published before 1961 takes effect from its publication date only if it then satisfied all of this Article's provisions; otherwise it is invalid.
  [Art. 75.7](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-7)
- **Recs. 75C–75D** *(advisory)*:
  - **75C** — Give the original author of a pre-1961 invalid designation the chance to correct it before anyone else designates a neotype.
  - **75D** — When validly designating a neotype after an earlier invalid pre-1961 attempt, prefer the specimen that attempt had already picked out.
- **75.8** — If, after a neotype has been designated, the presumed-lost former name-bearing type (holotype, syntypes, lectotype, or an earlier neotype) is found to still exist, publication of that discovery **automatically restores** the rediscovered material as the name-bearing type and sets the neotype aside — unless the Commission, on application, rules that the neotype is to be retained instead.
  [Art. 75.8](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-75-neotypes/#art-75-8)

## Article 76 — Type locality
Governs how the geographic (and stratigraphic) origin of a name-bearing type is determined — and, critically, that this value tracks the *current* name-bearing type, not a fixed textual claim.

- **76.1** — The type locality is the place of capture, collection or observation of the name-bearing type. Where the taxon has syntypes and no lectotype has yet been designated, the type locality spans every syntype's place of origin (Art. 73.2.3).
  [Art. 76.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-76-type-locality/#art-76-1)
  - **76.1.1** — If the name-bearing type (or its wild progenitor) was transported artificially before capture, the type locality is the place from which that unnatural transport began — not the point of eventual capture.
    [Art. 76.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-76-type-locality/#art-76-1-1)
- **Rec. 76A** *(advisory)* — to ascertain or clarify a type locality (or type horizon, type host), weigh in order of preference:
  - **76A.1.1** — data accompanying the original material.
  - **76A.1.2** — collector's notes, itineraries, or personal communications.
  - **76A.1.3** — the original description of the taxon.
  - **76A.1.4** — as a last resort, and without prejudice to other clarification, localities within the taxon's known range or from which referred specimens were taken.
  - **76A.2** — a type-locality statement later found erroneous should be corrected.
- **76.2** — **The place of origin of a validly designated lectotype becomes the type locality**, superseding any previously published type-locality statement — even one that pre-dates the lectotype designation.
  [Art. 76.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-76-type-locality/#art-76-2)
- **76.3** — **The place of origin of a validly designated neotype becomes the type locality**, likewise superseding any earlier published statement.
  [Art. 76.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-76-type-locality/#art-76-3)

**Practical consequence:** the type locality is not an independent fact about a name — it is *derived* from whichever specimen currently holds name-bearing status. It changes automatically, and retroactively supersedes prior statements, the moment a lectotype (Art. 76.2) or neotype (Art. 76.3) is validly designated. See **Database relevance** below.

**Common errors:** citing the original collecting locality as "the type locality" in a paper that also designates a lectotype from a syntype series collected elsewhere — after a valid lectotype designation the type locality is wherever *that specimen* originated, per Art. 76.2, not the composite locality of the original syntype series (Art. 73.2.3). Likewise, restating a pre-existing "type locality" field after a neotype designation without updating it to the neotype's origin (Art. 76.3) leaves the record wrong.

## Type specimen category reference

| Category | Name-bearing? | Governing Article | DwC `typeStatus` value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Holotype | **Yes** | Art. 73.1 | `holotype` | Sole name-bearing type fixed at original publication. |
| Syntype | **Yes** (collectively, until a lectotype is designated) | Art. 73.2 | `syntype` | Each syntype has equal status; the series *as a whole* is name-bearing. |
| Lectotype | **Yes** (from designation onward) | Art. 74.1 | `lectotype` | Selected from syntypes; supersedes them as sole name-bearing type. |
| Neotype | **Yes** (until superseded, Art. 75.8) | Art. 75.1 | `neotype` | Only valid when no prior name-bearing type is believed extant; reverts if that material resurfaces. |
| Hapantotype | **Yes** (it *is* the holotype, for extant protistans) | Art. 73.3 | `hapantotype` | Multi-preparation holotype, treated as indivisible (Art. 73.3.1). |
| Paratype | **No** | Art. 72.4.5 | `paratype` | Remaining type-series specimens once a holotype exists; ineligible for lectotype selection, eligible for neotype selection. |
| Paralectotype | **No** | Art. 74.1.3, 73.2.2 | `paralectotype` | Former syntypes stripped of that status once a lectotype is designated; do not revert if the lectotype is later lost. |
| Allotype | **No** — not even a formal Code category | Rec. 72A (advisory only) | `allotype` | Opposite-sex specimen to the holotype; usable label, no nomenclatural standing. |

**Terms with no standing under the Code:**
- **"Cotype"** — a historically ambiguous synonym for syntype or paratype; Rec. 73E advises against using it at all.
- **"Isotype" / "isosyntype"** — *botanical* (ICN) nomenclature terms; they have no meaning under the zoological Code and must not be used for animal type specimens.
- **"Topotype"** — a specimen from the type locality, not necessarily part of the type series; a Glossary/Recommendation-level convenience term (Rec. 75A) with no name-bearing function and no formal Code status as a type category.

## Database relevance
- Nomenclaturally, only *name-bearing* status matters — the table above is the operational split a specimen record needs, not the informal museum vocabulary layered on top of it. Allotype, cotype, and topotype carry no nomenclatural weight even though they appear routinely on labels and in collection databases.
- A specimen table needs (a) the type category from the table above, and (b) the nomenclatural act that conferred it: which publication, which author(s), which date, and — for lectotype/neotype — an explicit link to that designation as a dated act, not just a status flag. A single specimen can legitimately be a syntype under the original 1999-or-earlier publication *and later* become a lectotype under a distinct, dated designation act (Art. 74.1); modelling type status as one static field on the specimen loses that history and cannot represent "was, then became."
- Because type locality is *derived* from whichever specimen currently holds name-bearing status (Art. 76.2, 76.3), it must not be stored as a free-standing string on the taxon name — store it with a reference to the act (original publication, or the lectotype/neotype designation) that currently fixes it, so a later lectotype/neotype designation updates it traceably rather than leaving two conflicting localities in the record.
- Post-1999 lectotype (Art. 74.7) and neotype (Art. 75.3) designations have hard validity conditions; a database that merely records "designated lectotype/neotype in [year]" without capturing whether those conditions were met cannot distinguish a valid designation from a nomenclaturally inert one — flag designations lacking evidence of compliance rather than assume validity from a citation alone.
- Full nomenclatural-act and specimen-record schemas: see [references/data-modeling.md](data-modeling.md). Darwin Core term mapping (`typeStatus`, `typeStatusQualifier`, and related Type & Specimen Record Extension fields): see [references/dwc-mapping.md](dwc-mapping.md).
