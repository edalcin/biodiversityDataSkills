# Chapter 11 — Authors of Names and Nomenclatural Acts (Articles 50–51)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/authorship/ · 4th edition 1999

This chapter decides two separate questions that a database schema must keep separate: **who is credited as author of a name** (Art. 50), and **how that credit is written** in a citation (Art. 51). Land here when populating an `author` field, when the author of a work and the author of a name inside it diverge, or when deciding whether a name's author string needs parentheses.

## Article 50 — Authors of names and nomenclatural acts
Governs whose name attaches to a scientific name or nomenclatural act, independent of how it is later cited.

- **50.1** — The author of a name/act is whoever *first publishes it* ([Art. 3](03-publication.md)/[Art. 4](04-availability.md) publication) *in a way that satisfies the availability criteria* ([Arts. 10–20](04-availability.md)) — an exception applies to certain names first published in synonymy (50.7). This is a test on the act of making-available, not on general authorship of the containing work. [Art. 50.1](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-1)
  - Multi-author work: if the content shows only one contributor is responsible for the name/act, that person alone is the author; otherwise the whole work's author is deemed the name's author.
  - If no author/publisher can be determined from the content, the name/act is anonymous ([Art. 14](04-availability.md) governs availability of anonymous names).
  - **50.1.1 — the operative test:** if the content clearly shows a person *other than an author of the work* is solely responsible both for the name/act and for satisfying the availability conditions (other than the act of publication itself), that other person — not the work's author — is the name's author. If that person's identity isn't explicit in the work, authorship reverts to whoever published the work. **This is the rule that separates "author of the work" from "author of the name."** A name buried in someone else's paper (e.g. quoted verbatim from a letter, with clear attribution in the text) is authored by the letter-writer, not the paper's byline. [Art. 50.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-1-1)
  - **50.1.2** — original fixation of a type species by deliberate use of a species-group name under a prior misidentification: the person who deliberately so uses it is the author of a *new* specific name (cross-ref [Arts. 11.10, 67.13, 70.4](04-availability.md); [15](15-genus-group-types.md)).
  - **50.1.3** — this chapter applies equally to joint authors.
- **50.2** — A name made available in a meeting report/minutes is authored by whoever is responsible for the name, not the meeting's secretary/reporter. [Art. 50.2](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-2)
- **50.3 — authorship unaffected by rank/combination change:**
  - **50.3.1** — authorship of a family-group, genus-group or species-group name does not change when the taxon's *rank* changes. Exception: an infrasubspecific name otherwise meeting availability criteria that is later used in a way that makes it available at species/subspecies rank ([Arts. 10.2, 45.5.1](04-availability.md)) — its author is whoever first so uses it, not the original coiner.
  - **50.3.2** — a change of *generic combination* for a species-group name does not affect authorship of the specific name itself (contrast with the citation-formatting rule in [Art. 51.3](#article-51-citation-of-names-of-authors) below — combination change affects how the author is *written*, never who it *is*). [Art. 50.3](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-3)
- **50.4** — a *justified* emendation is credited to the author of the original incorrect spelling, not to the emender ([Arts. 19.2, 33.2.2](07-formation-spelling.md)). [Art. 50.4](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-4)
- **50.5** — an *unjustified* emendation is credited to whoever first publishes it, as its own available name ([Art. 33.2.3](07-formation-spelling.md)). [Art. 50.5](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-5)
- **50.6** — when identical names for the same taxon are published on the same date by different authors, precedence (and hence which author "owns" the name) is settled by the First Reviser mechanism of [Art. 24](06-validity-priority.md). [Art. 50.6](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-6)
- **50.7** — a name first published as a junior synonym (e.g. lifted from a museum label/manuscript) that became available before 1961 under [Art. 11.6](04-availability.md) is authored by whoever published it *as a synonym*, even if that publication credits a different originator — not by whoever later adopted it as the valid name. [Art. 50.7](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-50-authors-of-names-and-nomenclatural-acts/#art-50-7)

**Recommendations:** Rec. 50A (multi-author works — name explicitly only the contributor(s) directly responsible for the name, not every co-author of the paper); Rec. 50B (meeting secretaries should avoid introducing new names into minutes); Rec. 50C (attribute excluded/unavailable names to whoever published them with that status, unless that publication itself credits another originator). All advisory, not mandatory.

**Database relevance:** the `author` field of a name record is populated per Art. 50.1/50.1.1 — this may differ from the bibliographic "author" of the citing work. If your citation model has a single `publication.author` field reused for `name.author`, Art. 50.1.1 cases (name responsibility ≠ work responsibility) will be recorded wrong. Model `name.author` independently of `publication.author`, with the "B in A" convention of Rec. 51E as the display fallback, not the storage model.

## Article 51 — Citation of names of authors
Governs how an author's name is written once attached to a scientific name; distinct from Art. 50's question of who that author is.

- **51.1 — citation is optional.** The author's name is *not part of* the scientific name; citing it is customary and often advisable but never mandatory. [Art. 51.1](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-51-citation-of-names-of-authors/#art-51-1) **This is a Recommendation-level practice (Rec. 51A: cite the original author and date at least once per work), not a Code requirement** — a name string lacking an author citation is still a complete, valid name. Database schemas that treat an author field as `NOT NULL` are enforcing a convention the Code itself calls optional.
- **51.2 — form of citation.** The author's name follows the scientific name with no intervening punctuation; a cited year is separated from the author by a comma. [Art. 51.2](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-51-citation-of-names-of-authors/#art-51-2)
  - **51.2.1** — a *subsequent user's* name (as opposed to the original author), if cited, must be set off distinctively (e.g. "sensu Latreille" or "(as interpreted by Latreille)") — never in parentheses, which are reserved for the Art. 51.3 combination signal, and never simply appended as if it were the original author.
- **51.3 — the parentheses rule (changed combination).** When a species-group name is combined with a genus-group name *other than the one it was originally published in*, the original author (and date, if cited) are enclosed in parentheses: `Hymenolepis diminuta (Rudolphi, 1819)` for a species originally described as `Taenia diminuta Rudolphi`. [Art. 51.3](https://code.iczn.org/authorship/article-51-citation-of-names-of-authors/#art-51-3)
  - **The parentheses signal exactly one fact: the genus part of the binomen/trinomen currently differs from the genus in which the name was originally published.** They do not signal: a change of subgenus within the same genus, an emendation, a spelling correction, or a taxonomic opinion about validity.
  - **51.3.1** — parentheses are *not* used when the species-group name was originally combined with an incorrect spelling or an emendation of the generic name, even though an unjustified emendation is itself an available name with its own author/date ([Art. 33.2.3](07-formation-spelling.md)). Example pattern: a name first combined with a mis-spelled genus keeps no-parentheses citation even after the genus spelling is corrected, because the *corrected* spelling is the "original" generic combination for this purpose.
  - **51.3.2 — change of rank within the species group does not add/remove parentheses**, and neither does a subgeneric name in parenthetical position, transfer between subgenera of the same genus, or transfer of a subspecies to a different species *within the same genus*. Parentheses track genus identity only. The Code additionally *permits, but does not require*, appending the name of the person who made the new combination after the closing parenthesis (Rec. 51G: `Limnatis nilotica (Savigny) Moquin-Tandon`) — this combining-author suffix is a citation option, never a second author-of-record.
  - **51.3.3** — a narrow pre-1961 rule for species conditionally proposed in a new genus at the same time as their original description ([Art. 11.9.3.6](04-availability.md)): no parentheses when cited in the pre-existing genus, parentheses when cited in the conditionally-proposed genus.

**Recommendations:** Rec. 51A (cite original author+date at least once per work — advisory); Rec. 51B (transliterate non-Latin-alphabet author names); Rec. 51C ("et al." for 3+ joint authors, full list given elsewhere in the work); Rec. 51D ("Anon." for anonymous authorship, square brackets for inferred authorship); Rec. 51E ("B in A" form when the name's responsible party differs from the work's author — implements Art. 50.1.1 in citation form); Rec. 51F (flag nomenclatural status when citing unavailable/excluded names); Rec. 51G (append combining-author after the parentheses, per 51.3.2). All advisory.

**Database relevance:** **the parenthesised author-date string (e.g. `(Linnaeus, 1758)`) is a *derived* fact of the current name-in-combination, not a stored property of the name itself.** It is computed by comparing "genus of original publication" (fixed, from the protonym record) against "genus of current accepted combination" (mutable, changes with every generic transfer). Storing a literal `"(Linnaeus, 1758)"` string on a name/taxon row silently goes stale the moment the accepted genus placement changes — the parentheses must be (re)computed at combination-formation time, not persisted as text. Model: `protonym.original_genus`, `protonym.author`, `protonym.year`, and a `current_combination.genus`; render parentheses iff `current_combination.genus != protonym.original_genus` (Art. 51.3), suppressing them per the 51.3.1 incorrect-spelling exception. Cross-ref [`references/data-modeling.md`](data-modeling.md) for the protonym/combination relationship this implies, and [`references/10-species-group.md`](10-species-group.md) for the underlying species-group name model.

**Common errors:** (1) treating the author citation as part of the taxon name for matching/deduplication — Art. 51.1 makes it optional and non-diagnostic on its own; (2) applying parentheses on a subgenus change or an emendation — 51.3.1/51.3.2 explicitly forbid this; (3) conflating the combining author (Rec. 51G, optional, follows the parentheses) with the protonym author (inside the parentheses, mandatory format when cited).
