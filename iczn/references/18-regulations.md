# Chapter 18 — Regulations Governing this Code (Articles 85–90)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/ · 4th edition 1999
This short chapter (the Code's own closing provisions) fixes what the Code is called and who wrote it, when it took effect and what happened to earlier editions, which language versions are authoritative, what falls inside its temporal and textual scope, who has the final word on interpretation, and how the Code itself may be amended. Land here for questions about the Code's own authority and provenance rather than about a taxonomic act — including, centrally, **which text governs when there is any doubt** (Art. 87, below).

## Article 85 — Title and authorship
- **85** — The official title is INTERNATIONAL CODE OF ZOOLOGICAL NOMENCLATURE; its author is the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature (the body defined in Chapter 17). [Art. 85](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/article-85-title-and-authorship/#art-85)
- **Recommendations:** **Rec. 85A** (advisory) — when citing the Code, give the edition (the current one is the fourth), its authorship, the date of publication, and the publisher of the official text in the language cited (cf. Arts. 86.2, 87).

## Article 86 — Effective date and force of the Code
*Identified from cache file `regulations-governing-this-code__new-page-2.txt`; confirmed subject: the fourth edition's effective date and the status of the prior (1985, third) edition.*

- **86.1** — The fourth edition of the Code came into force on **1 January 2000**. [Art. 86.1](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/new-page-2/#art-86-1)
- **86.1.1** — If an author acted before 1 January 2000 under Art. 23.9 (reversal of precedence), 65.2.3, or 70.3 to preserve usage of a name, a later author may not set that action aside merely because it predates the fourth edition; if confirmation is needed, the Commission may confirm it without advance notice. [Art. 86.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/new-page-2/#art-86-1-1)
- **86.1.2** — Names or nomenclatural acts submitted for publication before 1 January 2000 under the third (1985) edition's rules, but not actually published until after 31 December 1999, are not invalidated for failing to meet the fourth edition's changed requirements; the Commission may validate them without advance notice on request. [Art. 86.1.2](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/new-page-2/#art-86-1-2)
- **86.2** — The **English and French texts of the Code are both official and equivalent in force, meaning, and authority** — the first statement of the equal-force principle that Art. 87 restates and extends. [Art. 86.2](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/new-page-2/#art-86-2)
- **86.3** — Rules from earlier editions of the International Rules / International Code of Zoological Nomenclature, and any amendments to them, have no force except to the extent they are reaffirmed in this (fourth) edition, and then only as expressed here — the fourth edition is self-contained; nothing carries over by mere historical continuity. [Art. 86.3](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/new-page-2/#art-86-3)
- **Recommendations:** **Rec. 86A** (advisory) — authors/editors of works submitted during 1999 were advised to ensure the work would meet the fourth edition's requirements, to avoid new names or type fixations becoming invalid solely for not having been published before the 1 January 2000 cutover (now of historical interest only).

## Article 87 — Official Texts
**This Article is the basis for every disclaimer in this skill: nothing here, or anywhere in this synthesis, has any nomenclatural authority. Only the Code's own official texts do.**

- **87** — The Commission may authorize publication of the Code in other languages under conditions it decides; every such authorized text is official and equivalent in force, meaning, and authority to the English and French texts (Art. 86.2). **If an apparent difference in meaning arises between official texts, the matter must be referred to the Commission, whose interpretation is final.** [Art. 87](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/article-87-official-texts/#art-87)

**Consequence for this skill, and for any reader relying on it:** the English and French texts of the Code are the only texts that carry legal force by default (plus any other language text the Commission has separately authorized). This file, `references/*.md` throughout this skill, and any other paraphrase, summary, translation, or synthesis — however carefully sourced — has **no interpretive authority whatsoever**. Where the exact wording of a rule matters (a manuscript review, a Commission submission, a genuine dispute over meaning), consult an official text directly via the linked URL, not this synthesis. Where two official texts appear to conflict, only the Commission can resolve it (Art. 87, last sentence) — that is not a determination any database, skill, or individual author can make unilaterally.

## Article 88 — Application of the Code
- **88** — No name or nomenclatural act published before 1758 enters zoological nomenclature (cf. Art. 3, the *Systema Naturae* 10th-edition starting point — see `references/03-publication.md`). Names, works, and nomenclatural acts published after 1757 are governed by this Code's provisions, even where they draw on information from earlier works. [Art. 88](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/article-88-application-of-the-code/#art-88)

## Article 89 — Interpretation of the Code
- **89.1** — In interpreting the Code, a word or expression defined in the Glossary takes the meaning given there for Code purposes (see `references/glossary.md`). [Art. 89.1](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/article-89-interpretation-of-the-code/#art-89-1)
- **89.1.1** — Any genuine doubt or difficulty over the meaning of a word or expression used in the Code or Glossary must be referred to the Commission, whose ruling is final — the same final-authority pattern as Art. 87 for text-language disputes. [Art. 89.1.1](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/article-89-interpretation-of-the-code/#art-89-1-1)
- **89.2** — Recommendations, Examples, and all Titles and Appendices are **not part of the Code's legislative (binding) text** — they are advisory or illustrative only. This is the textual basis for this skill's own Article/Recommendation distinction (see Constraint 3 in this skill's build notes, and every "Recommendations:" line across `references/*.md`, which is always marked advisory and never treated as a mandatory rule). [Art. 89.2](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/article-89-interpretation-of-the-code/#art-89-2)

## Article 90 — Amendments of the Code
- **90** — The Code can be amended only by the international body of zoologists holding the delegated power of the International Congresses of Zoology over the Commission (i.e. currently IUBS, per Art. 77), and then only on a recommendation from the Commission presented through, and approved by, that body's Section of Zoological Nomenclature. This is the full amendment procedure that Declarations (Art. 78.3.2–78.3.3) provisionally stand in for when a change is merely clarifying rather than major — a Declaration is not itself an Art. 90 amendment, only a provisional one pending ratification (Art. 80.1). [Art. 90](https://code.iczn.org/regulations-governing-this-code/article-90-amendments-of-the-code/#art-90)
