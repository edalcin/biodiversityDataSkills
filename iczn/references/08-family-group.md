# Chapter 8 — Family-Group Nominal Taxa and their Names (Articles 35–41)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/ · 4th edition 1999

Land here for anything about superfamilies, families, subfamilies, tribes, subtribes, or the validity of a family-group name whose type genus has become a homonym or a synonym. The chapter fixes the rank range covered by "family-group," states the Principle of Coordination for that group, and works through the four failure modes a family-group name can suffer through its type genus (homonymy of the genus name, suppression of the genus name, synonymy of the genus name, misidentification of the genus).

## Article 35 — The family group
Defines the rank range and the two rank-change effects that matter for record-keeping.

- **35.1** — The family group runs from superfamily down to any rank below superfamily and above genus (family, subfamily, tribe, subtribe, and further ranks as needed). Collective groups and ichnotaxa at this level are cross-referenced to Art. 10.3. [Art. 35.1](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-35-the-family-group/#art-35-1)
- **35.2** — Every rank in the family group is governed by the same rules except for the suffix required at each rank [Art. 29.2]; suffix aside, treat superfamily, family, subfamily, tribe and subtribe names identically. See Art. 36 for how coordination interacts with this. [Art. 35.2](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-35-the-family-group/#art-35-2)
- **35.3** — A family-group name's application is fixed by the type genus of the nominal taxon, not by current content or usage [Arts. 61–65]. [Art. 35.3](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-35-the-family-group/#art-35-3)
- **35.4** — Formation/treatment follows Art. 11.7 and Arts. 25–34.
  - **35.4.1** — A family-group name built on an unjustified emendation or incorrect original spelling of the type genus name must be corrected to match the correct genus-group name, unless preserved under Art. 29.5 or Art. 33.2.3.1/33.3.1. [Art. 35.4.1](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-35-the-family-group/#art-35-4-1)
  - **35.4.2** — If the type genus's unjustified emendation later becomes its own substitute name, the family-group name is rebuilt on that substitute name's stem; author and date of the family-group name do not change. [Art. 35.4.2](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-35-the-family-group/#art-35-4-2)
- **35.5** — After 1999, if an older family-group name is discovered at a lower rank (e.g. a subfamily name) than the name in prevailing usage at a higher rank in the same taxon (e.g. the family name), the older, lower-rank name does NOT displace the younger, higher-rank name — prevailing usage at the higher rank wins going forward. This is a stability override on strict priority, limited to post-1999 discoveries. [Art. 35.5](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-35-the-family-group/#art-35-5)

**Database relevance:** 35.5 means "priority order" alone cannot always compute the currently valid family-group name — a stability exception keyed to a discovery date (post-1999) and to "prevailing usage" (a usage judgment, not a computable fact) can override it. Do not hard-code family-group validity as a pure sort by original-publication date.

## Article 36 — Principle of Coordination
States the single most important structural fact for this whole chapter.

- **36.1** — A family-group name established at ANY one rank in the family group (superfamily, family, subfamily, tribe, subtribe, etc.) is deemed simultaneously established, by the same author and on the same date, for nominal taxa at every other rank in the family group. All these coordinate taxa share the same type genus; each name is formed from that type genus's stem with the rank-appropriate suffix [Art. 29.3, Art. 34.1]. Practical effect: publishing "HESPERIIDAE" in 1809 also establishes HESPERIOIDEA and HESPERIINAE that same year with the same author, even if no one used those exact words until decades later. [Art. 36.1](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-36-principle-of-coordination/#art-36-1)
- **36.2** — When a family-group taxon is raised or lowered in rank, its type genus does not change [Art. 61.2.2]. [Art. 36.2](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-36-principle-of-coordination/#art-36-2)

**Database relevance:** a single nomenclatural act at any family-group rank yields an entire coordinate SET of names (superfamily/family/subfamily/tribe/subtribe as applicable), all with identical author and date and the same type genus. A schema that stores "family name" and "subfamily name" as independently authored fields will misattribute authorship the moment only one rank's name was ever explicitly published — the unpublished coordinate ranks still legally exist with that same authorship from that same date. Model the coordinate set as derived from one authorship event plus a rank + suffix rule, not as N independent name records. See `references/data-modeling.md`.

## Article 37 — Nominotypical taxa
- **37.1** — When a family-group taxon is subdivided, the subordinate taxon containing the type genus of the taxon above it automatically carries the same name (suffix changed for rank) with the same author and date as the parent [Art. 36.1] — no separate act of establishment is needed or possible. This is the "nominotypical" taxon (e.g. TIPULINAE is the nominotypical subfamily of TIPULIDAE because Tipula is the family's type genus). [Art. 37.1](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-37-nominotypical-taxa/#art-37-1)
- **37.2** — If the name in use for a family-group taxon must be replaced (because unavailable or invalid, per Art. 23.3.5), the subordinate taxon containing the type genus of the new valid name becomes the nominotypical taxon under the new name; nominotypical status re-attaches to whichever taxon holds the type genus, not to whichever taxon held the old name. [Art. 37.2](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-37-nominotypical-taxa/#art-37-2)

**Database relevance:** never create an independent authorship/date record for a nominotypical subfamily/tribe/subtribe — it is not a separately published name at all; derive it from the parent taxon's name + type genus and flag it as nominotypical rather than storing separate bibliographic fields.

## Article 38 — Homonymy between family-group names
- **38.1** — This Article is a pure cross-reference: homonymy rules for family-group names live in Art. 39 (invalidity through the type genus) and Art. 55 (the general homonymy definitions and treatment). See `references/12-homonymy.md` Art. 55 for the operative rule; do not treat Art. 38 itself as stating a separate test. [Art. 38](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-38-homonymy-between-family-group-names/)

## Article 39 — Invalidity due to homonymy or suppression of the name of the type genus
- **39** — A family-group name is invalid if its type genus's name is (a) a junior homonym, or (b) wholly or partly suppressed by the Commission [Arts. 81.2.1, 81.2.2]. Replacement order: first, the next-oldest available synonym of the family-group name (including names of its own subordinate family-group taxa) under Art. 23.3.5; failing any such synonym, a new name built on the type genus's own currently valid name (whether that valid name is itself a pre-existing synonym or a newly coined nomen novum). [Art. 39](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-39-invalidity-due-to-homonymy-or-suppression-of-the-name-of-the-type-genus/) (Article 39 is a single unnumbered provision; there is no Art. 39.1)

**Database relevance:** a "type genus junior homonym" or "type genus suppressed" flag on the genus record should propagate a computed-invalid status to every family-group name built on it — this is a referential-integrity-style rule, not an independent editorial judgment per family-group name.

## Article 40 — Synonymy of the type genus
- **40.1** — If a family-group taxon's type genus name later turns out to be a junior synonym of another genus name, that alone does NOT invalidate or require replacing the family-group name. Synonymy of the type genus is not, by itself, grounds for renaming the family. [Art. 40.1](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-40-synonymy-of-the-type-genus/#art-40-1)
- **40.2** — Narrow historical exception: if a family-group name WAS already replaced before 1961 specifically because its type genus was found to be a synonym, and the replacement name is in prevailing usage, the replacement is to be kept (not reverted to the older name) despite Art. 40.1's general rule.
  - **40.2.1** — The kept replacement name retains its own (later) author, but takes over the PRIORITY DATE of the name it replaced — i.e. it is treated as the senior synonym for priority purposes even though a different, earlier name technically exists. Citation practice (Rec. 40A, advisory): cite as `Author, date (priority-date)`, e.g. THAUMALEIDAE Bezzi, 1913 (1847) — Bezzi's 1913 replacement name keeps its own authorship but inherits 1847's priority rank against ORPHNEPHILIDAE Rondani, 1847 and any later synonym. [Art. 40.2](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-40-synonymy-of-the-type-genus/#art-40-2) / [Art. 40.2.1](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-40-synonymy-of-the-type-genus/#art-40-2-1)

**Recommendations:** Rec. 40A — advisory citation format `Name Author, Year (PriorityYear)` for names preserved under 40.2.1; not a validity requirement.

**Database relevance:** Art. 40.2 is a trap for any schema that assumes "priority date = date the currently-valid name was published." A name-authority record needs TWO date fields for this case: `authorship_date` (Bezzi, 1913) and `priority_date` (1847, inherited from the replaced name) — collapsing them loses the legally correct priority ranking against competing synonyms. This is rare (pre-1961 cases only) but must not be modeled away as a data-quality anomaly.

## Article 41 — Misidentified type genera and overlooked type fixations
- **41.1** — If a family-group name's stability is threatened because the type genus itself was misidentified (interpreted differently than its own type species defines), or the type genus's type species was itself misidentified, or a valid type-species fixation for the type genus was simply overlooked by later authors, resolution is not automatic here — the rule is a pointer to Art. 65.2, which in some circumstances requires referral to the Commission rather than allowing a taxonomist to resolve it unilaterally. [Art. 41](https://code.iczn.org/family-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-41-misidentified-type-genera-and-overlooked-type-fixations/)

**Database relevance:** a case flagged under Art. 41 is a "requires Commission ruling" case, not a case a database can auto-resolve by algorithm — record it as a pending-status name, referencing any Commission Opinion once issued (see `references/17-commission.md`).
