# Chapter 15 — Types in the Genus Group (Articles 66–70)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/ · 4th edition 1999

This is the densest and most consulted type-fixation chapter: it governs the type species of every genus and subgenus. Land here to determine which nominal species is (or can become) the type species of a genus-group name, by which mechanism, and in what order those mechanisms are tried. Getting the precedence order in Art. 68 wrong is the single most common practical mistake reviewers see.

## Article 66 — Application
Scope of the chapter and the ichnotaxon carve-out.

- **66.1** — Applies to nominal genera and subgenera (including genus-group divisions deemed subgenera, Art. 10.4) but **not** to collective groups, which have no type species (Arts. 13.3.2, 42.3.1, 67.14). [Art. 66](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-66-application/#art-66)
  - An ichnotaxon at genus-group level named after 1999 must have a type species fixed to be available; one named before 2000 does not require one, though a type species may still be fixed under Art. 69 (see Art. 13.3.3, `references/04-availability.md`).

## Article 67 — General provisions
Foundational rules that apply regardless of how the type species was fixed.

- **67.1** — The name-bearing type of a genus or subgenus is a **nominal species** — the "type species" — not a taxonomic species concept (Art. 42.3). This is the single most important framing in the chapter: the type is a *name*, tied to its own name-bearing specimen(s), not whatever a later author decides the genus "really means." [Art. 67.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-67-general-provisions/#art-67-1)
  - **67.1.1** — A genus and its nominotypical subgenus automatically share the same type species (Art. 44.1, Art. 61.2 — `references/13-typification.md`); this coordination is structural, not a separate act.
  - **67.1.2** — The type species' name stays attached even if it later becomes a junior synonym, junior homonym, or a suppressed name.
- **67.2 — eligibility (originally included nominal species).** Only a nominal species *originally included* in the genus/subgenus when it was established is eligible for type-species fixation. [Art. 67.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-67-general-provisions/#art-67-2)
  - **67.2.1** — "Originally included" means cited in the original publication under an available name (including an incorrect spelling, Art. 67.6) as a species or subspecies, or cited there as a deliberate misidentification of an earlier name (Arts. 11.10, 67.13).
  - **67.2.2** — For a genus/subgenus established before 1931 (before 2000 for ichnotaxa) with no species originally included, the species *first subsequently and expressly included* are deemed the originally-included set.
  - **67.2.3** — A bare reference to a publication that happens to contain a species name does not count as expressly including that species.
  - **67.2.4** — Citing one genus-group name as a synonym of another does not transfer the synonym's species into the other genus.
  - **67.2.5** — A species cited only doubtfully/conditionally, as *species inquirenda*, or as *species incertae sedis* is deemed not originally included.
- **67.3** — Only nomenclatural acts and published statements made *at the time the genus/subgenus was established* count toward deciding valid type fixation (Art. 67.8/68) and the originally-included set (Art. 67.2).
- **67.4** — Type-species fixation is **original** if made in the work that establishes the genus/subgenus (Art. 68), or **subsequent** if made afterward (Art. 69).
  - **67.4.1** — A genus-group taxon established after 1930 (after 1999 for an ichnotaxon) *must* fix its type species originally — subsequent fixation is not available to it (Art. 13.3).
- **67.5 — "designation" construed rigidly.** Not every mention of a species amounts to a type designation. Excluded: (67.5.1) citing a species merely as an example; (67.5.2) citing a character as "typical" of the genus; (67.5.3) an ambiguous or conditional statement.
- **67.6** — If the type species' name is cited using an incorrect spelling or an unjustified emendation, the fixation is deemed made under the species' correct original spelling.
- **67.7** — An author's error in attributing authorship/date to the type species or to the genus, or in citing the first express inclusion, does not invalidate an otherwise valid fixation — as long as the species itself was eligible.
- **67.8 — replacement names (*nomina nova*) inherit the type species.** If a genus-group name is published expressly as a new replacement name for a prior name, or replaces a prior name via unjustified emendation (Art. 33.2.3), **the prior name and its replacement share one type species**: fixing the type species for either one fixes it for both, regardless of any contrary statement in the replacement's publication. This mirrors the *nomen novum* rule for available names generally (Art. 60.3, `references/06-validity-priority.md`). [Art. 67.8](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-67-general-provisions/#art-67-8)
  - **67.8.1** — The inherited type species must still be eligible under Art. 67.2 for the *prior* name.
- **67.9 — misidentified type species.** If a validly fixed type species is later found to have been misidentified, Art. 70.3 governs the fix (below) — this article does not itself provide the remedy, it just cross-refers.
- **67.10** — When two or more genus-group taxa merge into one taxonomic genus, each nominal taxon keeps its own type species unchanged; the valid name of the merged genus follows ordinary priority (Art. 23, `references/06-validity-priority.md`).
- **67.11** — A nominal species already serving as type species of one genus can also be fixed as type species of another; when that happens, the two genus-group names become objective synonyms (Art. 61.3.3, `references/13-typification.md`).
- **67.12** — For a genus-group name first published as a synonym and later made available under Art. 11.6.1 (pre-1961), its type species is the nominal species first directly associated with it at that time.
  - **67.12.1** — If more than one species was so associated, all of them count as "originally included" for Arts. 68–69 purposes.
- **67.13** — If a type species is fixed using a species that was originally included as a deliberate, expressly-stated misidentification of an earlier name, the type species so fixed is treated as a **new nominal species** in its own right (Arts. 11.10, 50.1.2, 70.4; for its own name-bearing type see Art. 72.4.2).
  - **67.13.1 / 67.13.2** — Covers, respectively, original and subsequent designation of such a deliberately-misapplied name as type species.
- **67.14** — If a genus-group name is later repurposed as a *collective-group* name, its type species is disregarded for as long as it is used that way (Art. 23.7).

## Article 68 — Type species fixed in the original publication
The precedence order among fixation mechanisms is the load-bearing rule of the whole chapter.

- **68.1 — order of precedence.** When a species qualifies under more than one mechanism, the valid fixation is decided in this fixed order: **(1) original designation → (2) monotypy → (3) absolute tautonymy → (4) Linnaean tautonymy.** A lower-ranked mechanism never overrides a higher one that also applies. [Art. 68.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-68-type-species-fixed-in-the-original-publication/#art-68-1) *(Rec. 68A, advisory: cite only the valid fixation, not every mechanism that happens to apply.)*
- **68.2 — original designation.** If the establishing author explicitly designates one originally-included nominal species as type species, that is the type species by original designation (subject to the misidentification remedy in Art. 70.3). [Art. 68.2](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-68-type-species-fixed-in-the-original-publication/#art-68-2)
  - **68.2.1** — Before 1931, the phrase "gen. n., sp. n." (or equivalent) applied to only one of several new species originally included counts as an original designation, if no other type was explicitly designated.
  - **68.2.2** — If no explicit designation was made but one originally-included new species bears the species-group name *typicus/-a/-um* or *typus*, that species is deemed the type by original designation.
- **68.3 — monotypy.** If the genus/subgenus was established for a single taxonomic species denoted by one available name, that species is automatically the type species — regardless of cited synonyms, subspecies, unavailable names, or the author's private belief that more species belonged in the genus. [Art. 68.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-68-type-species-fixed-in-the-original-publication/#art-68-3)
  - **68.3.1** — If a new genus is split into subgenera at the moment of establishment and the nominotypical subgenus contains only one species, that species is the type by monotypy of the new genus.
- **68.4 — absolute tautonymy.** If a species-group name (or one of its cited synonyms) originally included in the genus is spelled identically to the genus-group name itself, the species denoted by that identical name is the type species. [Art. 68.4](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-68-type-species-fixed-in-the-original-publication/#art-68-4)
- **68.5 — Linnaean tautonymy.** For a genus-group taxon established **before 1931 only**: if the synonymy of just one originally-included species cites a pre-1758, one-word name identical to the new genus name, that species is the type species by Linnaean tautonymy. [Art. 68.5](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-68-type-species-fixed-in-the-original-publication/#art-68-5)
- **68.6** — Fixation using a deliberately-cited misidentification/misapplication is governed by Arts. 11.10 and 67.13, not by this article directly.

**Database relevance:** `fixation_mechanism` on the type-species record must be an enum tracking Art. 68.1's four values plus "subsequent designation" and "subsequent monotypy" (Art. 69, below) — and the enum's ordinal order should mirror 68.1's precedence so validation logic can check that a lower-precedence mechanism wasn't wrongly claimed when a higher one applied. Monotypy and tautonymy are **structurally different claims**: monotypy fires when exactly one species was included at all; absolute/Linnaean tautonymy fire regardless of how many species were included, based purely on name-identity with the genus name. Do not merge them into one "automatic" flag.

## Article 69 — Type species not fixed in the original publication
Applies only to genus-group taxa established before 1931 (before 2000 for ichnotaxa) that reach Art. 68 with no valid original fixation.

- **69.1 — subsequent designation.** The *first* author after establishment to designate one of the originally-included nominal species as type species fixes it validly; no later designation by anyone is valid, however reasonable. [Art. 69.1](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-69-type-species-not-fixed-in-the-original-publication/#art-69-1)
  - **69.1.1** — Even without using the word "designate," an author who states (rightly or wrongly) that a species "is the type" or equivalent, and clearly accepts it as such, is deemed to have made a valid subsequent designation.
  - **69.1.2** — A subsequent designation first appearing in a literature-recording (e.g. cataloguing) publication is accepted, if otherwise valid.
- **69.2 — eligibility for subsequent fixation.** An originally-included species remains eligible even if it is already the type species of another genus-group taxon (Art. 67.11).
  - **69.2.1** — Designation via an unjustified emendation or incorrect spelling of an eligible species' name is deemed a designation of that species under its correct spelling.
  - **69.2.2** — Designating a species that was *not* originally included, while simultaneously placing it in synonymy with exactly one originally-included species, fixes that one originally-included species as type.
  - **69.2.3** — Designating a type species denoted by a nomen novum for an originally-included species' name fixes that originally-included species as type.
  - **69.2.4** — Designating a species that was originally included as an expressly-stated misidentification fixes, as type, the *actual taxonomic species* the misidentification denotes — not the nominally cited name.
- **69.3 — subsequent monotypy.** If a genus/subgenus was established with no included species, and exactly one species was ever first subsequently included, that species is automatically fixed as type species by subsequent monotypy.
- **69.4 — no fixation by elimination.** Removing every originally-included species but one from a genus does **not** by itself fix the survivor as type species — an affirmative act under 69.1–69.3 is still required.

**Recommendations:** Rec. 69A (advisory, ten numbered criteria 69A.1–69A.10) — guidance for *choosing among* eligible candidates when a subsequent designator has discretion (prefer well-described/well-preserved material, "virtual tautonymy," common/economically important species, etc.). None of these are mandatory; they only steer a discretionary choice that Arts. 69.1–69.3 have already authorized.

## Article 70 — Identification of the type species
Resolves disputes about *which* taxonomic species a validly-fixed type species name actually denotes.

- **70.1** — Correct species identification is assumed, absent clear contrary evidence, whenever an author includes a previously-established species in a new genus/subgenus, or fixes it as type species.
- **70.2 — overlooked fixation.** If an earlier, overlooked type-species fixation surfaces, that earlier fixation controls and any later ones are invalid; if this would cause instability or confusion, refer the case to the Commission.
- **70.3 — misidentified type species: the author's explicit choice.** If a type species turns out to have been misidentified when fixed (excluding deliberate misidentifications under Arts. 11.10/67.13/69.2.4, which are handled differently), the discovering author gets to choose, for stability and universality, **either**: [Art. 70.3](https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-genus-group/article-70-identification-of-the-type-species/#art-70-3)
  - **70.3.1** — retain the nominal species previously cited as type species (i.e., keep the name as originally fixed), **or**
  - **70.3.2** — select instead the taxonomic species actually involved in the misidentification — but only by expressly invoking Art. 70.3 and citing *both* the name previously cited as type species and the name of the newly selected species.
  - Neither option is a default; the choice must be made and documented. This is also the sole exception, alongside Arts. 74–75 and the Commission's plenary power, to the otherwise-immutable type-fixation rule of Art. 61.1.3 (`references/13-typification.md`).
- **70.4 — deliberate misapplication (not misidentification).** For a type species fixed (70.4.1, originally) or designated (70.4.2, subsequently) using a species deliberately and expressly cited as a misidentification of an earlier name, the rules are instead Arts. 11.10, 67.13, and 69.2.4 — a different track from ordinary 70.3 misidentification because the misapplication was intentional and on the record, not a later-discovered error.

**Database relevance:** A type-species record should carry a `misidentification_resolution` field with values {none, retained-70.3.1, replaced-70.3.2} plus the citing author/publication, because 70.3 is an author *choice*, not an automatic rule — two different authors could in principle make different choices before one becomes established usage. Objective synonymy derived from shared type species (Art. 61.3.3) must be recomputed whenever a 70.3.2 replacement changes which taxonomic species a type species record actually points to.

**Common errors:** Applying monotypy when more than one species was originally included (it requires *exactly* one); invoking absolute/Linnaean tautonymy while ignoring that a higher-precedence original designation already exists (68.1 order); treating a post-1930 genus as eligible for subsequent designation (Art. 67.4.1 forecloses that); and confusing ordinary 70.3 misidentification with the deliberate-misapplication track of Arts. 11.10/67.13/69.2.4, which are handled entirely differently.

See also `references/16-species-group-types.md` for the specimen-level type-fixation mechanisms one rank down, and `references/data-modeling.md` for the shared type-fixation schema.
