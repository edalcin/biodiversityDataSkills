# Chapter 10 — Species-Group Nominal Taxa and their Names (Articles 45–49)

Source: https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/ · 4th edition 1999

Land here for anything about species and subspecies names: whether an old "variety"/"form"/"aberration" name is available at all (Art. 45.6 — this single provision decides availability for a very large fraction of 19th/20th-century names), coordination between species and subspecies rank, what happens to a name's authorship when a species is moved to a new genus, and why a misidentified name can never "become" the available name for the thing it was mistakenly applied to.

## Article 45 — The species group
Defines the rank range and, critically, the availability test for names below subspecies rank.

- **45.1** — The species group encompasses exactly two ranks: species and subspecies [see also Art. 10.2]. [Art. 45.1](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-1)
- **45.2** — Species and subspecies names follow identical rules except where an Article names only one rank explicitly (interpolated names denoting aggregates of species/subspecies are separately addressed at Art. 6.2). [Art. 45.2](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-2)
- **45.3** — A species-group name's application is fixed by the name-bearing type [Arts. 61, 71–75] of the nominal taxon as denoted by the exact combination in which the name was originally established — not by later reassignment of that type. [Art. 45.3](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-3)
- **45.4** — Formation/treatment follows Art. 11 and relevant parts of Arts. 19, 20, 23–34. [Art. 45.4](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-4)
- **45.5** — A name EXPRESSLY proposed for an entity below subspecies rank ("infrasubspecific," see Glossary in `references/glossary.md`) is not available unless Art. 45.6 says otherwise; it falls outside the species group entirely and outside Code regulation [Art. 1.3.4]. A fourth name tacked onto an existing trinomen (species + subspecies + one more word) is automatically infrasubspecific by virtue of that position alone — UNLESS that fourth word is instead an interpolated species-group name under Art. 6.2, which is not treated as an addition to the trinomen. [Art. 45.5](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-5)
  - **45.5.1** — An infrasubspecific name can never become available later merely through someone treating it as if it were higher-ranked (e.g. "elevating" it) — only a Commission ruling can do that. If a later author independently applies the same word validly to a species or subspecies (making it available under Arts. 11–18), that later use creates a NEW name with the later author's own authorship and date, even if that later author chooses to credit the original infrasubspecific publication. [Art. 45.5.1](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-5-1)
- **45.6** — **Determination of subspecific vs. infrasubspecific rank of a name following a binomen.** Default: a name following a binomen is subspecific. It is infrasubspecific only under one of these enumerated exceptions:
  - **45.6.1** — Infrasubspecific if the author expressly gave it infrasubspecific rank, OR the work's content unambiguously shows it was proposed for an infrasubspecific entity (cross-refer 45.6.4 for the pre-1961 "variety"/"form" carve-out). [Art. 45.6.1](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-6-1)
  - **45.6.2** — Infrasubspecific if the author used the term "aberration," "ab." or "morph" — regardless of publication date. [Art. 45.6.2](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-6-2)
  - **45.6.3** — Infrasubspecific if first published AFTER 1960 and the author expressly used "variety"/"var."/"forma"/"v."/"f." ("form"). Post-1960 use of these terms is a hard infrasubspecific marker. [Art. 45.6.3](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-6-3)
  - **45.6.4** — **Subspecific (i.e. AVAILABLE) if first published BEFORE 1961 and the author expressly used "variety"/"form" (or "var."/"forma"/"v."/"f."), UNLESS** the author also expressly gave it infrasubspecific rank, or the work's content unambiguously reveals an infrasubspecific intent (in which case fall back to 45.6.1 and it IS infrasubspecific). This is the pivotal rule: pre-1961 "var." and "form" names are presumptively AVAILABLE at subspecies rank unless the author's own words or the work's content say otherwise. [Art. 45.6.4](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-6-4)
    - **45.6.4.1** — Rescue clause: even a name that Art. 45.6.4 would otherwise leave infrasubspecific is deemed subspecific from its ORIGINAL publication date if, before 1985, it was either (a) adopted by some author as the valid name of a species or subspecies, or (b) treated as a senior homonym. Adoption/treatment before 1985 retroactively confers subspecific availability back to the original date. [Art. 45.6.4.1](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-45-the-species-group/#art-45-6-4-1)

**Database relevance:** Art. 45.6 is a decision tree a taxonomic name-authority database MUST encode explicitly to compute `availability` for any pre-1985 name carrying a rank-word like "var.," "variety," "forma," "form," "ab.," "aberration," or "morph." Required fields to make 45.6 computable per name: `original_publication_year`, `rank_term_used` (free text or controlled list: variety/form/aberration/morph/none), `author_expressly_stated_rank` (subspecific/infrasubspecific/unstated), `content_unambiguous_infrasubspecific` (boolean, requires literature review — not automatable), and for the 45.6.4.1 rescue clause, `pre_1985_adoption_as_valid_name` (boolean + citation). Do NOT collapse this to a simple "has a trinomen-style rank word → infrasubspecific" heuristic: for pre-1961 var./form names the DEFAULT under 45.6.4 is the opposite (available), and post-1960 the default flips (45.6.3, unavailable) — the cutoff years 1961 and 1985 are both load-bearing and easy to get backwards.

## Article 46 — Principle of Coordination
- **46.1** — A name established at either rank in the species group (species OR subspecies) is deemed simultaneously established by the same author, on the same date, for a taxon at the OTHER rank; both the species-rank and subspecies-rank taxa share the same name-bearing type, whether that type was fixed originally or only subsequently. Practical effect: describing a new species automatically also establishes its (as yet unnamed) nominotypical subspecies with identical authorship and date. [Art. 46.1](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-46-principle-of-coordination/#art-46-1)
- **46.2** — Raising a subspecies to species rank, or lowering a species to subspecies rank, never changes its name-bearing type [Art. 72.1.2, Art. 61.2.2], regardless of whether that type was fixed originally or subsequently. [Art. 46.2](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-46-principle-of-coordination/#art-46-2)

**Database relevance:** as with the family group (`references/08-family-group.md` Art. 36) and the genus group (`references/09-genus-group.md` Art. 43), the species/subspecies pair produced by one authorship event is a coordinate PAIR — same author, same date, same name-bearing type — not two independently authored records. A species record and its nominotypical-subspecies record must derive authorship/date/type from the single originating publication event, not accept two separately entered citations.

## Article 47 — Nominotypical taxa
- **47.1** — When a species is treated as containing subspecies, the one containing the species' own name-bearing type is denoted by the SAME species-group name as the species, with the same author and date [Art. 46.1] — this is the "nominotypical subspecies" (e.g. *Papilio machaon machaon*). [Art. 47.1](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-47-nominotypical-taxa/#art-47-1)
- **47.2** — If the species name in use (and hence its nominotypical subspecies name) must be replaced under Art. 23.3.5 (unavailable/invalid), the subspecies containing the name-bearing type of the newly valid species name becomes the new nominotypical subspecies — nominotypical status follows the type, not the old label, and a differently-named subspecies established in the interim does not become nominotypical merely by seniority. [Art. 47.2](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-47-nominotypical-taxa/#art-47-2)

**Database relevance:** the nominotypical subspecies is a derived record (species name repeated at trinomial rank, sharing the species' type), not a manually authored one; recompute it if the species-level valid name changes (47.2) rather than leaving a stale nominotypical flag on the wrong subspecies epithet.

## Article 48 — Change of generic assignment
- **48.1** — An available species-group name, changed in gender ending if the new genus requires it [Art. 34.2], simply becomes part of a new binomen/trinomen whenever combined with a different generic name. The species-group name's own author and date NEVER change when the genus placement changes — moving *Turdus migratorius* to a different genus and calling it *Planesticus migratorius* does not create a new name "migratorius (Author, date)"; the combination is a re-pairing of an existing available epithet with a different genus, not a new nomenclatural act. [Art. 48](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-48-change-of-generic-assignment/#art-48-1)

**Database relevance:** this is the single most consequential rule in this chapter for schema design. A species-group NAME (epithet + its own author/date/type) and a COMBINATION (that epithet paired with a particular genus at a particular time) are two different things and must be two different entities/tables, not one flattened "current scientific name" string. If a database stores only the current accepted binomen, it silently loses the original combination and original authorship the epithet was established under — this is exactly the gap Darwin Core's `originalNameUsage`/`originalNameUsageID` terms exist to close, alongside `scientificNameAuthorship` (which must record the ORIGINAL author/date, parenthesized under Art. 51/Rec. 51A conventions when the genus has changed — see `references/11-authorship.md`). See `references/data-modeling.md` for the name-vs-combination entity split and `references/dwc-mapping.md` for the field-level mapping.

## Article 49 — Use of species-group names wrongly applied through misidentification
- **49.1** — If a previously established species-group name was applied to a taxon only because of a MISIDENTIFICATION (i.e. the author thought they had taxon A but actually had different taxon B), that misapplied name does NOT thereby become an available name for taxon B — even if A and B later end up in, or are reassigned to, different genera. The one exception: a misidentification deliberately and knowingly used as the basis for fixing a NEW genus's or subgenus's type species is handled separately under Arts. 11.10 and 67.13, not voided by this Article. [Art. 49](https://code.iczn.org/species-group-nominal-taxa-and-their-names/article-49-use-of-species-group-names-wrongly-applied-through-misidentification/#art-49-1)

**Database relevance:** a "misidentification" record (an author's use of name X for what turns out to be a different taxon) must never be modeled as if it minted a new available name for the misidentified taxon — flag it as a taxonomic/nomenclatural note (a `nomenclatural_status` value such as "misidentification," distinct from synonym or valid name) attached to the CORRECT name's synonymy list, not as its own name-bearing record, except in the narrow Art. 11.10/67.13 deliberate-type-fixation case.
